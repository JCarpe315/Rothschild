## PART 0 — HOW TO READ THIS MAP

The trilogy documents connections in prose; this volume renders them as a **graph**. The rules of the other volumes carry over, with graph conventions added:

- **Nodes** are people, families, firms, states, or institutions. Each is registered in Part 1 with its class and pedigree.
- **Edges** are documented relationships, typed by mechanism (Part 2). An edge exists in this map **only where the trilogy documents it** — with the pedigree grade of its evidence at the point of listing.
- **Myth-corrupted nodes** (Part 1, class M) are mapped *as claims*: the node exists in the public record, but the documented connection is an absence, a correction, or a refutation. Mapping the false edges is part of mapping the network, because the myth system itself is a network — one with documented origins (Dairnvaell 1846; Drumont 1886; Mullins 1952) and documented carriers.
- **Eras** are given so the map can be read longitudinally: the network of 1815 is not the network of 1914, and neither is the network of 2026.
- **Degree notes:** where an edge's evidence is contested or thin, the edge carries a flag rather than a silent inclusion.

## PART 0A — THE FIVE-MINUTE VERSION (PLAIN LANGUAGE)

Before the tables, the story. A **network** is just this: people and institutions as **points**, and their documented relationships as **lines**. If you drew your own family this way — you, your parents, your spouse's family, your employer, your bank — every line would be a real, checkable fact. This map does that for the Rothschild world, with one strict rule: *a line exists only where the documents exist.* No line is drawn because someone famous said so.

**One example, walked slowly.** In 1770, Mayer Amschel Rothschild married Gutle Schnapper. That's one line — a marriage line. Gutle's father, Wolf Salomon Schnapper, was a small Frankfurt banker who happened to do business with a man named Buderus, who managed the fortune of the Landgrave of Hesse — then the richest prince in Germany. In 1790, Schnapper introduced Buderus to his son-in-law. That's a second line — an introduction. Within a decade, Mayer Amschel was handling the prince's money across Europe. That is what this map calls an **edge**: not a rumor of influence, but a documented connection with a date and a source, through which something — money, information, access — demonstrably flowed.

Now scale that up. This volume draws well over a hundred such edges, connecting the best part of two hundred points: the five Rothschild houses, thirteen intermarried families, a dozen allied banking families, twenty-seven states, the agents and court officials in between, the corporations they financed, the newspapers they leaned on, the regulators who later investigated them — and, separately and clearly marked, the *false* connections (the Illuminati, the Federal Reserve "ownership," the space laser), mapped as claims with their real history attached, because you cannot understand the family's story without understanding the legend that shadowed it.

**Three plain promises:** every line carries its evidence grade ([A] strongest, [X] hostile myth); every technical word is defined where you first meet it, and again in the word-list at Part 2A; and wherever the evidence is thin or missing, the line says so instead of pretending.

## PART 2A — THE WORDS THIS MAP USES (PLAIN DEFINITIONS)

- **Node** — one point on the map: a person, family, firm, state, or institution.
- **Edge** — one documented line between two points, with a date and a source.
- **Pedigree tag** — the reliability grade printed right after each claim. **[A]** = archives, court records, official documents. **[S]** = scholarly work. **[B]** = serious secondary sources. **[C]** = lead-grade, single-source. **[X]** = hostile myth literature — quoted only to show what the myth is and where it fails.
- **Correspondent banking** — banks doing business through each other: one bank accepts another's paper, moves its money, vouches for its client. Before telephones, this *was* international banking — a web of favors and accepted IOUs.
- **Underwriting** — guaranteeing that a government's loan will be placed with investors; the guarantor takes the risk and charges the fee.
- **Certification** — the family's core business in one word: their name on a loan made the loan trustworthy, and they charged states for that trust (Volume III explains the machinery).
- **Apex creditor** — not the owner of the network, but the point everyone eventually routes through: the bank that can always pay.
- **Syndicate** — a group of banks sharing one big loan.
- **Agency / proxy** — a local representative bound to the house: the family's man in a city where it has no office (Belmont in New York, Bleichröder in Berlin).
- **Enforcement** — regulators, courts, and prosecutors: the counter-network that only appears on the map from the 1930s onward, and thickens sharply after 2015.
- **Betweenness** (used once, in Part 4) — how often a point lies *on the path* between other points. High betweenness means "the person in the middle" — everyone needs them to reach everyone else.
