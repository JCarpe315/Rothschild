## PART 0 — HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT

Before we tell the story, we need to agree on how we're going to tell it, because this particular story has been told badly — sometimes maliciously — more often than almost any other family history in Europe. Two centuries of propaganda, pamphlet wars, Hollywood films, and internet folklore sit on top of a genuinely documented record. So this document operates on a simple forensic principle:

**We do not adopt the theories of others. We gather clues and evidence.**

Every claim in this document carries a pedigree tag that tells you exactly how well it is supported:

- **[A] — Archive-grade / primary-adjacent.** Drawn from official family records, municipal archives, gravestone inscriptions, tax registers, or the Rothschild family's own archive and its published scholarly instruments.
- **[S] — Scholarly.** Peer-reviewed work or major reference scholarship (encyclopedias, university-press monographs, the *Neue Deutsche Biographie*, the *Encyclopaedia Judaica*).
- **[B] — Quality secondary.** Serious biographies, museum scholarship, and local-history scholarship with named sources.
- **[C] — Crowdsourced or single-source.** Genealogy websites, hobbyist DNA projects, commercial heraldry databases. Treated as *lead-grade*, never adopted.
- **[L] — Lore.** Family traditions, legends, and stories that circulate without documentary support. Documented here because they are part of the record, but clearly fenced off from evidence.
- **[X] — Hostile myth.** Material from antisemitic conspiracy literature. We document these claims because they are historically important — they shaped public perception for two centuries — but we never repeat them as fact, and where they are false we show *why* they are false.

A second principle: **anomalies are data.** Where sources contradict each other — and they do, frequently — we do not pick the prettiest answer. We log the conflict, show the evidence on each side, resolve it where the evidence allows, and leave it honestly open where it does not. Part 11 of this document is a complete anomaly register: thirty-two conflicts investigated and dispositioned, with the open residue precisely stated, and a precise map of what physical archive would settle each one.

A third principle: **nothing is omitted.** That includes the parts of this story that are embarrassing to every side: the family's own self-mythologizing, the nineteenth-century gutter press, the twentieth-century propaganda ministries, and the modern internet's copy-paste folklore. All of it is in here, sorted.

### A note on why this family, and why this method

The Rothschilds are not just a family. They are a case study in how modern Europe processed three anxieties at once: money, mobility, and Jewish emancipation. A family that lived behind a locked gate in a German ghetto in 1744 became, within two generations, the creditors of nations. That velocity made them a screen onto which everyone projected their hopes and terrors. Understanding what is *documentably true* about their origin is therefore not just genealogy — it is a calibration exercise in how history gets made, distorted, and weaponized.

## PART 1 — THE EVIDENTIARY FRAME: WHERE THE DOCUMENTED RECORD BEGINS

Here is the first and most important fact of this entire document, stated plainly:

**The documentable, source-verifiable history of the Rothschild banking family stops in sixteenth-century Frankfurt. Everything before that dissolves into fragments.**

The best scholarly ceiling of what can be firmly asserted is Niall Ferguson's two-volume *The House of Rothschild* (1998–99), which was written with a privilege no other historian has ever been granted: full access to the family archives in London, Paris, and Frankfurt **[S]**. Even Ferguson begins his account in the Frankfurt of Mayer Amschel's lifetime (1744–1812), not with the ancestors. The reason is structural: the family's own published genealogical instruments are the work of a specific scholarly tradition introduced in Part 1 — the "Frankfurt school" of Jewish genealogy — whose foundation documents are these:

1. **Horovitz, *Die Inschriften des alten Friedhofs der israelitischen Gemeinde zu Frankfurt a.M.* (1901)** — a 768-page edition of every legible Hebrew gravestone inscription in Frankfurt's medieval Jewish cemetery, with German translations **[A]**.
2. **The Frankfurt Memorbuch, published by Aron Freimann and Meier Hildesheimer in the *Festschrift zum 70. Geburtstage A. Berliners* (Frankfurt, 1903)** — the community's book of remembrance, recording deaths by name, date, and (for the wealthy) with epitaphs **[A]**.
3. **Aron Freimann, *Stammtafeln der freiherrlichen Familie von Rothschild* (Kumpf & Reis, Frankfurt, 1906)** — the scholarly family tables, privately printed in 25 copies, built from the gravestones and Memorbuch, with inscriptions translated into German **[A]**.
4. **Alexander Dietz, *Stammbuch der Frankfurter Juden* (1907)** — genealogical register of 625 Frankfurt Jewish families from 1349 to 1849, with a street plan of the Judengasse **[A/S]**.
5. **Ettlinger's *Ele Toldot*** — a 32-volume handwritten transcription of the Frankfurt Jewish burial records from 1241 to 1824, preserved on microfilm at the Leo Baeck Institute (MF 536) and the Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People in Jerusalem **[A]**.

Everything asserted about the sixteenth- and seventeenth-century generations in this document rests on this five-piece foundation, cross-checked against tax registers, the community's *Judenstättigkeit* (the ghetto statute book), and municipal records.

The family's own archive is also candid about a trap. The Rothschild Archive itself warns: "Rothschild was by no means a unique name and there were many other families called 'Rothschild' originating in Frankfurt" **[A]**. House-sign names were shared by unrelated families living under the same sign. Genealogists untrained in this system routinely merge families that merely shared a house name. The 1905 *Jewish Encyclopedia* went further, asserting that the scattered Rothschilds of Frankfurt, Worms, and elsewhere were "little doubt... one family," based on the recurrence of distinctive given names (Kalman, Jacob) — a plausible but reconstructed claim **[S]**. We treat each branch separately and note the connections only where a marriage or a record documents them.

Finally, the archive maintains that "the most detailed published genealogy of the Rothschild banking family is *Le Sang des Rothschild* by Joseph Valynseele and Henri Mars (Paris, 2004)" **[A]** — with one crucial caveat: that work *begins with Mayer Amschel*. The pre-Mayer chain's best published anchors remain the Freimann/Dietz/Berghoeffer instruments of 1901–1925. The origin of the family, in other words, is *less* documented than everything that came after — which is precisely why it needs forensic care.

### The knowledge-evolution wall, documented

One of the most instructive things about this family's history is watching the *boundary of the known* move over time. We can document it exactly:

- **1883** — the Danish genealogist G. L. Wad could write that Mayer Amschel's father was "so obscure that the present doesn't even know his name" **[B]**.
- **1889** — the *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* names the father as **Amschel Moses Rothschild** and supplies the skeleton of the family story **[S]**.
- **1901–1907** — Dietz, Freimann, Horovitz, and Kracauer push the documented chain back four more generations **[A/S]**.
- **1924/25** — Christian Wilhelm Berghoeffer, the family's own Frankfurt archivist, publishes the first archive-based biography, with gravestone numbers and Memorbuch folios for every generation **[A]**.
- **1972** — the *Encyclopaedia Judaica* publishes its authoritative family entry, extending the pedigree to "Uri (d. c. 1500), first recorded member of the family" **[S]**.
- **1973** — under Victor, 3rd Lord Rothschild, a detailed family tree is commissioned, reaching back to "Uri Feibesh in 1450," drawing on Horovitz, the Memorbuch, the burial society record (*sefer ha-Kahranim*), and — intriguingly — the **parish registers in the State Archives of Wiesbaden** **[A/B]**.
- **2005** — the *Neue Deutsche Biographie* (Manfred Pohl) consolidates the accepted prehistory **[S]**.

One more witness marks the boundary from below: Lewi's economic history of the 1930s still states that "**the most diligent researches have failed to establish anything about Amschel Moses**" — which means Berghoeffer's 1924/25 biography, not the decades around it, was the true watershed. Everything before Berghoeffer on this family is prehistory in the strict sense; everything after him stands on his archive work **[B]**.

The open questions in Part 14 map precisely onto the seams of this evolution.

## PART 2 — THE NAME BEFORE THE FAMILY: "AT THE RED SHIELD"

### 2.1 House-signs: the naming system you need to understand first

German towns did not number their houses until the modern era. Frankfurt introduced house numbers only in **1759** **[A: ADB]**. Before that, a house was known by a sign hung on its facade — a red shield, a green jar, a golden goose, a ship, an owl. These signs hung perpendicular to the walls, "like inn signs," and some stretched across the narrow street itself, so that the ghetto's skyline was a jangle of iron and painted boards **[B: Kriegk 1869; Lewi]**.

For the Jews crammed into Frankfurt's ghetto — the **Judengasse**, literally "Jews' Lane" — the sign was not decoration. It was address, identity, and eventually surname. When families were forced to adopt fixed surnames (a 1807 requirement in Frankfurt, and similar edicts across German lands), many simply froze their house-sign into a family name. That is the entire origin of the name "Rothschild": **German *zum rothen Schild* — "at the red shield."** The original pronunciation was "ROTE-shilt"; the English "Roths-child" is a spelling pronunciation **[B: Ancestry surname research]**. The *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* already recorded this derivation in 1889; Graetz had given it in volume X of his *History of the Jews* in the 1870s **[S]**.

Why this matters: because once you understand that "Rothschild" was originally an *address*, you understand why the banking family shares the name with completely unrelated Frankfurt families, why myths about secret name-changes are structurally absurd, and why every generation of genealogists has had to separate the banker's line from name-alikes.

### 2.2 The name in the wild, 1313–1338: before the family existed

The red-shield house-sign name circulated in German lands long before any Rothschild ancestor used it **[C: single-source, lead-grade]**:

- **1313** — *Hartmut zum Rotenschite* (or *Rotschilt*) recorded in **Speyer**.
- **1338** — the form *Rotscilt* registered in **Lübeck**.

These come from a commercial heraldry database and have not been confirmed against the Speyer and Lübeck municipal archives; we flag them as **lead-grade only**. They are still useful: they demonstrate that the name-form existed as a house-name across German cities a full two centuries before the banking family, which is consistent with everything else we know — and they foreclose nothing, because none of these bearers is claimed as an ancestor.

### 2.3 The Danish tradition: a family legend with a real etymological kernel **[L]**

Berghoeffer — the family archivist himself — begins his 1924/25 biography with the following admission: **"Man weiß nicht, woher die Familie Rothschild nach Frankfurt eingewandert ist" — "It is not known whence the Rothschild family immigrated to Frankfurt"** **[A]**.

What he documents instead is a *family tradition*: the Rothschilds themselves believed they came from **Roskilde, Denmark**, taking their name from that city because in German Roskilde was sometimes called "**Rothschild**." The Danish genealogist Wad recorded the same tradition in 1883, citing Graetz **[B]**.

Here is the wonderful complication: the tradition's *etymology is partly real*. Roskilde genuinely carried the German exonym "Rothschild" — Hans Christian Andersen even footnoted it in his fairy tale "Little Tuk" ("Roeskilde... falsely called Rothschild") **[B]**. So a family could indeed have been "the Rothschilds" — "the Roskilde people" — without ever having seen a red shield. But the tradition fails on the documented evidence: the banker's line demonstrably takes its name from a Frankfurt house, and there is no documentary trace of the family in Denmark. The tradition is recorded here, exactly as Berghoeffer recorded it, as a *belief*, not a fact. Berghoeffer also mentions that the family's own librarian (himself, in the first edition) had floated possible Danish roots — showing how seriously the family itself once weighed it **[B]**.

### 2.4 House-name to surname: the general pattern

Kriegk's 1869 account in the *Gartenlaube* (a mass-circulation illustrated magazine) gives the fullest contemporary description of the system: Frankfurt ghetto families took their surnames from their houses — *Bär* (bear), *Haas* (hare), *Hahn* (rooster), *Hirsch* (deer), *Hecht* (pike), *Kann*, *Ochs* (ox), *Reuß*, *Rindsfuß* (ox-foot), *Schiff* (ship), *Schloß* (castle), *Sichel* (sickle), *Stern* (star), *Stiefel* (boot), *Strauß* (ostrich) — and, of course, *zum rothen Schild* **[B]**. The Frankfurt family of the future banker's line lived, before it was "Rothschild," under **rooster signs**: *zur roten Rose* (at the red rose) and *zum roten Hahn* (at the red rooster). This is the hinge of the whole prehistory, and the next section is about it.

## PART 3 — THE HAHN LAYER: THE FAMILY BEFORE IT WAS ROTHSCHILD

### 3.1 What the sources agree on

Multiple independent strands of evidence — Amos Elon's *Founder* (1996), the Frankfurt Jewish Museum's METAhub project, the Rothschild Archive, and Berghoeffer — converge on this picture **[S/B/A]**:

- The banker's ancestors were **a branch of the Jewish Hahn family, resident in Frankfurt's Judengasse from about 1500–1530**.
- The Hahn family's house was the **Rote Rose** (red rose), at the southern end of the ghetto, created around the end of the fifteenth century.
- Around **1542**, the Rote Rose was subdivided, creating the neighboring house **zum roten Hahn** (at the red rooster), which became the Hahns' main residence for a long period **[B: METAhub house histories]**.
- In the early seventeenth century the family produced a significant rabbi: **Juspa (Joseph) Hahn**, a Frankfurt dayan (religious judge) who died in 1637 and whose ethical will/chronicle *Josif Ometz* was published posthumously; he chronicled the Fettmilch uprising of 1614–16; his ethical will *Josif Ometz* was published by his son-in-law in 1723 **[B: alemannia-judaica, citing Prof. Aron Freimann, 1937]**. His full name carries a second migration fossil — **Juspa Nörlinger (Nörlingen) Hahn**, the middle name possibly from Nördlingen — paralleling the Schwelm element: this was a family whose names recorded its walks across the German lands.
- The family later split into branches that took other names. METAhub states it plainly: **"the most famous Jewish family of Frankfurt, namely the Rothschild family, descends from the Hahn family"** **[B]**.
- The old Hahn line itself remained **poor for centuries** — small shopkeepers, linen dealers, peddlers; banking success came only after 1800, and the (unrelated-name) Hahn family's later wealth is marked in Frankfurt by their donation of the Merkurbrunnen (Mercury fountain) **[B: METAhub]**.

### 3.2 Friedberg: the origin claim of the greatest Frankfurt genealogist **[B, lead-grade]**

Aron Freimann — the scholar who edited the Memorbuch, compiled the 1906 *Stammtafeln*, and was the greatest authority on Frankfurt Jewry — wrote in a 1937 local-history study of Friedberg: **"Die Familie Hahn wird wohl aus Friedberg nach Frankfurt eingewandert sein, sie war mit der gleichfalls aus Friedberg stammenden Familie Rothschild verwandt"** — "The Hahn family probably immigrated to Frankfurt from **Friedberg**, and was related to the likewise Friedberg-originating Rothschild family" **[B: alemannia-judaica, quoting Freimann]**.

This is the single most specific pre-Frankfurt origin claim on record, and it comes from *the* authority. Friedberg (Wetterau, Hesse) had a documented Jewish community from **1241**, with a monumental thirteenth-century mikveh and a Judengasse parallel to the main street; at times its Jews may have outnumbered the Christian population **[B: alemannia-judaica Friedberg]**. We grade the claim **lead-grade** — "wohl" ("probably") is Freimann's own hedge — but note its structural elegance: the family's later rabbinic branch held the post of **Landesrabbiner (district rabbi) of Würzburg *and Friedberg*** (see §4.3), exactly the region Freimann names. Two independent vectors — the Hahn-origin claim and the rabbinic line's Friedberg office — point at the same Wetterau landscape between Frankfurt and Wiesbaden.

### 3.3 Schwelm: a branch name and a town-shaped clue

The Hahn family also produced a branch called **Hahn-Schwelm** — "the Schwelm Hahns." The Frankfurt City Museum's house history records the Hahn-Schwelm family at the Rote Rose from the end of the fifteenth century, and notes that branches spreading to other houses included the one that became the Rothschilds **[B: METAhub]**. "Schwelm" is a town in Westphalia; German-Jewish surname scholarship (Menk's dictionary) lists "Schwelm" among town-derived family names, and the De Gruyter monograph on early-modern Frankfurt Jewry documents a **Schwelm family in its house from 1594 to 1744** with tax-register values **[S/B]**. Whether the Hahn-Schwelms originated in the Westphalian town is **inference-grade** — no document has been found that walks the family from Schwelm to Frankfurt — but the name itself is a fossil of migration, and we preserve it as a lead.

### 3.4 "Since 1530": the family's own arrival marker

The sources repeatedly give **1530** as the date the Hahn family (or the family's direct ancestors) appear in Frankfurt: Elon's account (as cited by German Wikipedia: "the ancestors were a branch of the Jewish Hahn family, resident in the Judengasse since 1530"), the Jewish Museum's Hahn family history ("The Hahn family lived in Frankfurt since 1530"), and a 1989 Jewish Telegraphic Agency feature ("the patriarch Isaac son of Elchanan came to Frankfurt in 1530") **[S/B/B]**. Against this, the Rote Rose itself was occupied at the end of the fifteenth century **[B]**. The honest summary: **the family was present in the ghetto by the early sixteenth century, possibly from the 1490s.** The 1530 date is a tradition-anchored marker, not a document-anchored one.

## PART 4 — THE DOCUMENTED CHAIN: URI TO ISAAC ELCHANAN (c.1450/1500–1585)

Now the ground firms. Every generation from here to Mayer Amschel is anchored in a named gravestone, a Memorbuch folio, or a tax register. We present the chain as Berghoeffer's 1924/25 table gives it — with his gravestone numbers (from Horovitz's 1901 inscription corpus) and Memorbuch folios — cross-checked against the *Encyclopaedia Judaica* (1972), the NDB (2005), and Kober's history **[A/S]**.

### 4.1 Uri (c.1450/1500) — the first recorded member

- **Uri Feibesh** (also rendered Uri Freibesch) is the first identified ancestor **[A: 1973 commissioned family tree; EJ2]**.
- The **1973 tree** — commissioned under Victor, 3rd Lord Rothschild, drawing on Horovitz, the Memorbuch, the burial-society record (*sefer ha-Kahranim*), and the Wiesbaden parish registers — dates him **to 1450** **[A]**.
- The **Encyclopaedia Judaica** (1972), the scholarly standard, dates him **d. c. 1500** and calls him "first recorded member of the family" **[S]**.
- Berghoeffer's 1925 table opens with "**Uri (um 1500)**" — Uri, around 1500 — and notes that according to Dietz his wife was **Fogele Worms**, a Worms-born woman **[A/B]**.

**Resolution of the date split:** the 1450 date belongs to a family-commissioned tree; the c.1500 date to scholarly consensus. Both are recorded; we use **c.1450–1500** with the weight on c.1500. No document of Uri's life is cited anywhere; he stands at the edge of the reconstructable. (The Wiesbaden parish-register dependency of the 1973 tree is discussed in Part 8 — it is the most intriguing open lead in the whole file.)

### 4.2 Elchanan (d. 21 April 1546) — the oldest *attested* member

- Uri's son **Elchanan** is **"the oldest attested member of the family in Frankfurt"** — attested by his **gravestone, No. 187 in Horovitz's corpus**, death date **21 April 1546** **[A: Berghoeffer/Kober]**.
- Conflicting date: several reconstructed trees (Streun; Merkel-Zeller) give his death as **1550**. We weight **1546**, because it is the gravestone-cited date in the two oldest authorities; 1550 is crowdsourced **[C]**.
- His wife per Dietz was **Fogele Worms** (d. 1550 in the reconstructed trees) — a woman of **Worms**, the great Rhineland Jewish center of the ShUM league, where the family would later place its most distinguished rabbi. The Worms connection, appearing already in this generation's marriage, will recur like a leitmotif (see the network map, Part 8).
- Corti (1928), without gravestone apparatus, already knew the essentials: "his father's tombstone simply bearing the name **Elchanan**" **[B]**.

⚠ **Anomaly 3.1 — the 21 April echo.** Berghoeffer's table records father Elchanan dying **21 April 1546** (gravestone 187) and his son Isaak Elchanan dying **21 April 1585** (gravestone 301) — the same day-of-month exactly thirty-nine years apart. Both dates carry Horovitz gravestone numbers, so we transmit both as recorded; but the coincidence is suspicious enough to log. Possibilities: a genuine Hebrew-date coincidence (same date in the Jewish calendar is a normal, even esteemed, coincidence); a transcription echo somewhere between the cemetery and Horovitz's edition. **Unresolvable without inspecting the gravestone corpus directly** — flagged, both dates weighted as recorded.

### 4.3 Isaac Elchanan (d. 21 April 1585) — the man who became "Rothschild"

Everything about the family's name converges on this man.

**Identity and dates.** Isaac Elchanan (Isaak Elchanan), son of Elchanan, grandson of Uri. Death: **21 April 1585** (gravestone No. 301, Horovitz) **[A: Berghoeffer; S: EJ2, Corti]**. A minority tradition gives **13 October 1588**; we have found no source for that date and treat 1585 as established (see Anomaly Register #2). Reconstructed trees give a birth of 1528 **[C — unverified]**.

**The house.** He built, or acquired, the house **"zum roten Schild"** at the southern end of the Judengasse — the house that gave the family its name. The date is a genuine four-way conflict, and we document all four:

| Claim | Source | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| House built **1530s** | Rothschild Archive timeline | [A] family archive, but inconsistent with the attested builder |
| House built **~1563** | Frankfurt museum house history (METAhub) | [B] museum scholarship |
| Family resident **from 1567** (he "built a house... named zum roten Schild," living there with his wife Esther from 1567) | Berghoeffer 1924/25 | [A] archive-based biography — **strongest narrative source** |
| House **acquired 1576** | *Neue Deutsche Biographie* (Pohl, 2005) | [S] |

**Resolution.** The most defensible statement: the house existed and the family was in it by **1567**; formal acquisition is dated **1576** by the NDB. The 1530s claim is probably the family tradition of *when the Hahn/Rose lineage settled that end of the lane*; METAhub's 1563 and the occasional 1569 (a local popular source) bracket the same decade. Why might NDB say "acquired" rather than "built"? Because of what follows.

**The wife — and the father-in-law who may have owned the shield first.** Isaac Elchanan's wife was **Esther, daughter of Moses**, died **11 February 1609** (recorded in Berghoeffer's table; reconstructed trees give her birth as ~1530 **[C]**, and a variant tradition attaches her to the house signs *zur Leiter* / *zur Reuse* — at the ladder, at the snare — **[C]**, both recorded because every variant of the founder's wife's identity belongs in the file). Here a sixty-year-old scholarly puzzle resolves itself. The 1905 *Jewish Encyclopedia* reported that "the earliest notice of a member of the family, given in the burial records of Frankfort, is that of **Moses Rothschild (b. c. 1550), whose daughter Esther died in 1608**" **[S]**. Note the pieces: a "Moses," born around 1550, whose daughter died 1608/9. Berghoeffer's Esther died **1609** — Jewish and civil calendars straddle years — and she was **daughter of Moses**. The JE's "earliest notice of a member" was therefore **not a Rothschild man at all, but Isaac Elchanan's father-in-law**. And the NDB's careful verb "acquired" (1576) now reads differently: a man acquiring, in his fifties, the house attached to his wife's family — plausibly her father Moses's house. The Archive's "1530s" date may preserve exactly this: the house was **Moses's**, built or taken on when he married (c.1530s), passed down through Esther. We present this as a **documented-dates-plus-inference** synthesis: the house's pre-1576 history belongs to Esther's line, and the name "Rothschild" crystallized when her husband's family became identified with it.

**The tax record.** Isaac Elchanan appears in the Frankfurt Jewish tax assessments of **1567–1580 with a fortune of 2,700 gulden** — comfortable but not wealthy; a well-to-do tradesman, not a financier **[A: Berghoefter, citing the *Schatzung* registers; earlier sources cite a 1558 register with the same figure — the register year itself is one of our logged minor conflicts]**. For scale: this puts him in the ghetto's middle class. The Rothschild wealth is a much later event.

### 4.4 The fork: two sons, two destinies

Isaac Elchanan and Esther had (at least) two sons, and the family bifurcated into the line the world knows and a rabbinic line the world forgot. The 1912 family tree (published by the family; preserved and reproduced in the German Wikipedia article on Isaac Elchanan, with that article's own warning that the tree is "partly incorrect") gives the structure **[A/C]**:

**Line A — the banking line's trunk:**
- **Moses Isaac** ("Moses zum roten Schild," b. 1560 – d. **19 July 1635**), described as a **cantor** — a synagogue singer/official, which tells us the family's status and income were modest. Married **16 August 1593 Gutlin, daughter of Naftali** (d. 21 August 1641, gravestone 691). The 1912 tree adds that he later lived at the *Gelber Ring* ("yellow ring," another house). **[A: Berghoeffer table; C: 1912 tree]**
- His son **Naftali Hirz (Herz)** — the next section's subject.

**Line B — the rabbinic line:**
- **Elchanan "Hähnchen"** (d. April 1619/20), who lived at the Red Shield and married **Eva Wetzlar** in December 1585 — the first documented Wetzlar marriage (see the network map).
- His son **Rabbi Isaac (Isaak) Rothschild**, d. **10 January 1652**, a rabbi and head of the Frankfurt Jewish community, resident at the Red Shield from 1610; married **1608 to Kele, daughter of Mendl Oppenheim of Worms** (d. 31 March 1639, Frankfurt) — the first documented Oppenheim-of-Worms marriage.
- His daughter **Reizche** (d. 1688) married **Todros Nathan Oppenheim "zur silbernen Kanne"** (at the silver pot) — a second Oppenheim marriage.
- His son **Solomon**, **Landesrabbiner (district rabbi) of Würzburg and Friedberg** — the Friedberg office again.
- Solomon's son **Rabbi Menahem Mendel Rothschild** (b. ~1650 Frankfurt, d. **October 1731**, Worms) — the family's most distinguished pre-banking figure: preacher in Prague, from 1686 district rabbi of Bamberg and rabbi of Bayreuth and Baiersdorf, from 1718 in Hesse, finally rabbi of **Worms** until his death; a man of wealth; two daughters. He appears in the Worms *Pinkas* (community record book). And here is the detail that ties the whole prehistory together: the *Jewish Encyclopedia* records that Mendel "to his name adds **sometimes the surname Rothschild and sometimes that of Bacharach**" **[S]**. The family that would be "Rothschild" was, in its rabbinic branch, still also "Bacharach" — the name of the Rhineland rabbinical dynasty (from the town of Bacharach) that the reconstructed genealogies attach to the line (see §6.1 and the DNA thread, Part 9).
- Mendel's line continued in the rabbinate: a **Salomon Rothschild**, rabbi in Oettingen, and **Boas Raphael Rothschild**, author of the rabbinic work *Oni he-Leb Yam* (Fürth, 1766) — the rabbinic branch's last documented Frankfurt-connected figure, publishing in the very town where young Mayer Amschel would attend yeshiva **[B/C]**.

**The "one family" question.** The 1905 *Jewish Encyclopedia* asserted "there is little doubt that all the Rothschilds form one family," citing the recurrence of the distinctive names Kalman and Jacob **[S]**. Our documented synthesis: the banking line (descending from Moses Isaac) and the rabbinic line (descending from Elchanan Hähnchen) demonstrably share **Isaac Elchanan (d. 1585)** as common ancestor — the 1912 tree shows the fork, and both branches' gravestones sit in the same cemetery under Horovitz's numbers. Beyond Isaac Elchanan, "all Rothschilds everywhere" is reconstruction. The famous *Bacharach* surname-dynasty claim (descent from the medieval Worms/Mainz rabbinical Bacharachs, via "Uri Feibesch Bacharach") exists only in reconstructed trees **[C]** — documented here as unproven but preserved, because it matters to the DNA question (Part 9).

⚠ **Anomaly 3.2 — Mendel's death date.** The 1912 tree prints "**Mendel Rothschild (gest. 1782), Rabbi in Bamberg, Prague and Worms**." The *Jewish Encyclopedia* and the Worms Pinkas give **October 1731** (JE: born ~1650). A man born ~1650 dying in 1782 at age 132 is not credible; **1782 is an error** (possibly a transposition of 1732, the Pinkas year). Resolved: **1731/32**.

## PART 5 — THE DESCENT: FROM NAFTALI HIRZ TO AMSCHEL MOSES (1634–1755)

Five generations separate the name's founder from the banking dynasty's founder. Each is a named, dated, documented person. The pace of this section is deliberate: these are the generations no novel ever mentions, and they are the story.

### 5.1 Naftali Hirz (b. ~1592, d. 24 February 1685) — the man who carried the name to a new house

- Son of Moses Isaac the cantor; born around 1592 (some sources 1596) **[A/C]**.
- Married **3 May 1634 Schönche, daughter of Kalonymos Kaz** (d. 10 November 1661, gravestone 879, Memorbuch fol. 40 — the Kaz marriage matters; see the network map); after her death he married **Hanna** (d. 20 May 1686) **[A: Berghoeffer table]**.
- Died **24 February 1685** (Memorbuch, fol. 42, Freimann edition) **[A]** — note: several popular sources give 1 March 1685; the Memorbuch date is weighted.

**The move to the Hinterpfann — Anomaly 1 (the deepest open conflict in the file).** The *Hinterhaus zur Pfanne* ("back house at the pan," a rear tenement of the Pfanne house) stood near the northern entrance of the Judengasse, at **No. 188** in the 1759 numbering (one popular source says 139 — an error; the Archive and the Frankfurter Personenlexikon say 188). The family left the Red Shield for it — and this is the moment, Berghoeffer writes, when the family "from now on no longer [called itself] *zum roten Schild*, but **Rothschild**" **[A]**. A house-name had become a family name.

But *when*? Three documented answers conflict:

| Date | Source | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **1634** | Streun; the German Wikipedia family article; **Backhaus (1997, p. 80)** — "the Red Shield was only inhabited by Rothschild's ancestors until 1634" | modern scholarship + reconstruction |
| **1664** | **The Rothschild Archive itself**: "when his grandson Naftali Hirz moved to the Hinterpfann... in **1664**, he took the name Rothschild with him" | family archive |

**Harmonization (inference-grade, documented):** Naftali Hirz married on 3 May 1634 — the very year Backhaus and Streun give. It is entirely plausible he married *at the Red Shield*, was widowed in 1661, and moved to the Hinterpfann as a widower in 1664, dying there in 1685. That reading reconciles the marriage record, the Archive's date, and the widowhood — but it *is* a harmonization, not a document. **Status: the marriage is 1634 (documented); the move is 1634 per Backhaus/Streun and 1664 per the family Archive; our register keeps both, weighted toward the Archive for the move itself, with the harmonization noted as plausible.** This matters beyond trivia: the family's own claim is that the *name* dates from this move, and the name's documentary appearance is independently dated by the Archive timeline to "the 1650s" (consistent with either).

**The house.** The Hinterpfann was a poor person's house: a **1.70-meter-wide facade**, three stories plus attic, roughly 120 m² of usable space in total, which the Rothschilds shared with the **Bauer family** — Elon's reconstruction has the two families totaling ten to twelve people, though later accounts describe periods of up to thirty people under the roof when the wider family squeezed in **[S/B]**. It had been first mentioned in 1552 and was rebuilt in 1601 and again after the great ghetto fire of 1711 **[A/B: Streun; Berghoeffer]**. It was a fractional-ownership property — the Rothschilds held a **three-eighths share**, one more index of ghetto real-estate arithmetic. And the 1634 move was made, in Streun's account, "**with two brothers**" — the Hinterpfann was a clan settlement, not a nuclear household. Mayer Amschel was born in this house in 1744.

### 5.2 Kalman (Kalmann) Rothschild, "genannt Bauer" (d. 19 July 1707) — the alias that launched two centuries of myth

- Son of Naftali Hirz; died **19 July 1707**; assessed in **1690 at 6,000 gulden** — a modest but real rise; traded in **silk and wool (by 1700)** and was recorded as a **money-changer (1703)** **[A: Berghoeffer, citing tax registers]**. Money-changing — *Wechseln*, exchanging the dozens of currencies of the fractured German lands — is the ancestral trade; everything later grows from this skill.
- Married **Gitle, daughter of Joseph of Höchst** (d. **31 August 1664**, gravestone 917, Memorbuch fol. 6) **[A]**.

⚠ **The "Bauer" file — Anomaly 11, resolved.** Berghoeffer's table records this man, in the Frankfurt registers, as **"Kalmann Rothschild genannt Bauer"** — "Kalman Rothschild, called Bauer." His son appears the same way: **"Moses Kalmann Rothschild genannt Bauer."** The family's own archivist transmits the alias. Combined with three further documented facts — the **Bauer family co-residing in the Hinterpfann** (Elon) **[S]**, Amschel Moses's nickname in the community ("**das Bäuerchen**," little Bauer) **[A: Berghoeffer]**, and young Mayer Amschel's own infant nickname — the entire "Bauer myth" resolves into something stranger and more interesting than either side of the internet debate believes. The conspiracy literature claims the family was "originally named Bauer and changed its name to Rothschild in 1744" — presented as proof of hidden identity **[X]**. The documented truth: **the family was never named Bauer; but it was *called* Bauer in Frankfurt records for generations** — probably from the co-resident Bauer family of the Hinterpfann (families sharing a house were easily conflated by registrars). The myth is false as stated; its kernel is a real, documented alias. No name-change ever occurred: the name "Rothschild" descends from a house the family left in the seventeenth century.

### 5.3 Moses Kalman Rothschild, "genannt Bauer" (d. 19 October 1735) — the man who bought Joseph Süß Oppenheimer's paper

- Son of Kalman; died **19 October 1735** (Memorbuch fol. 43; the tax register lists) **[A]**. The 1912 tree's alternative date of 1765 is an error (he could not be taxed in registers of 1725–1750 if he died in 1765 — the registers list him; resolved: **1735**).
- **Wives — Anomaly 8, resolved.** Berghoeffer's table, with gravestone numbers, gives **two** wives: (1) **Vogel, daughter of Elchanan Waag, the tax collector** (d. 18 December 1713, Memorbuch fol. 43); (2) **Schönche, daughter of Wolf Buxbaum, physician** (d. 13 March 1733). An independent 1930s account (Lewi) confirms the same sequence from the same tradition. The 1912 tree instead shows a "**Särche Lechnich (d. 1768)**" as his wife — but also lists a *different* "Sarchen Lechnich" as the wife of his grandson Moses Amschel. **Resolution: the 1912 tree duplicated/garbled one woman across two generations.** The Lechnich wife belongs to the younger generation (see §5.4). The "d. 1768" is part of the garble — Amschel Moses's Schönche Lechnich died **29 June 1756** (gravestone 2579, Memorbuch fol. 45).

The wider clan, for the record: genealogical lists give Moses Kalman **as many as nine siblings**, including a **Herz Moses (d. 1728)** — the founder's great-uncle generation extended through much of the ghetto's family web **[C — list-grade, consistent with the community's size]**.

**The Süß Oppenheimer transaction — the single most loaded sentence in the prehistory.** The *Neue Deutsche Biographie* (Pohl, 2005) states, citing Dietz: **Moses Kalman acquired, between 1733 and 1735, bills of exchange worth 38,000 florins from Joseph Süß Oppenheimer, the Württemberg court factor** — the most famous, and most infamous, court Jew of the age, executed at Stuttgart in 1738 **[S]**. Berghoeffer quotes the same passage from Dietz, adding the context that at his peak Süß "did far bigger business with the first Frankfurt houses — Bernard, Brentano, d'Orville, Metzler, Grimmeisen" **[A]**. And a Czech university thesis records the family's tradition that Amschel Moses (Moses Kalman's son) had social contact with Süß in the Judengasse **[C]**.

Why does this matter so much? Because it places the family, documented, inside the court-Jew financial system **one generation before anyone called them court Jews** — and on the receiving end of the era's most notorious court Jew's paper. When Süß fell, the Frankfurt houses that held his bills were exposed; Moses Kalman had already absorbed 38,000 florins of them. The NDB does not say whether the paper was honored at par, discounted, or lost — that is one of our archive-bound open questions. But the connection itself is not conjecture: it is in Germany's national biography. (For the full meaning of "court Jew," see Part 6.4; for Süß's fate and its afterlife, Part 10's lore registry touches the myth machinery that later wrapped itself around his name too.)

### 5.4 Amschel Moses Rothschild, "genannt Bauer" (c. 1710 – 6 October 1755) — the founder's father

- Son of Moses Kalman; **died 6 October 1755** (gravestone 2854, Memorbuch fol. 45) in the **smallpox epidemic** that gutted the ghetto that year; his wife followed on **29 June 1756** — orphaning eleven-year-old Mayer within nine months **[A: Berghoeffer; S: EJ2]**. (A popular Jewish-education site gives 1757 for his death; that is an error — resolved.)
- Occupation: **money-changer and dealer in coins, and supplier of coin to the court of Hesse** — the same trade as his father, now touching a prince's treasury **[A/G]**.
- Tax assessment of **1749: 1,375 gulden** — less than half of what Isaac Elchanan commanded two centuries earlier in real status terms; the family, by the eve of the dynasty, was ghetto-comfortable, not rich **[A]**.
- Married **Schönche, daughter of Meier Lechnich** (d. 29 June 1756) **[A]** — the Hufeisen (horseshoe) house family. Alexander Dietz, the ghetto's genealogist, described the Lechnichs as **"penniless"** — secondhand clothes dealers; by mid-century Herts Lechnich had worked up to 5,000 gulden **[B: METAhub Hufeisen house history, citing Dietz]**. The founder's *mother's* people were the ghetto's poor; his *father's* people were modest money-changers. There is no aristocratic blood, no hidden fortune, no exotic origin in this family tree. That is the documented truth, and it is more remarkable than the myth.
- **A physical artifact survives him:** a small Talmudic tractate **hand-copied by twelve-year-old Amschel Moses in 1721**, possibly as scribal training — preserved today in the YIVO Institute's collections **[A: YIVO highlight]**. The boy who copied tractates became the coin dealer; his son became the banker. The artifact is the hinge.
- The couple had **eight children, five survived**: two older daughters, then **Moses Amschel (b. 1736, d. 23 December 1794)**, **Mayer Amschel (b. 1744)**, a younger brother **Kalman Amschel (b. 1754, d. 1782)** — briefly Mayer's partner — who married **Bunle Schames**, and a younger sister **Gutelche/Gitta (b. 1755, d. 1812)** **[A/B: Berghoeffer; Geni-derived lists agree on the core]**. One demographic note preserved from the modern scholarship: this family's child mortality ran **~58% above the Frankfurt average** — so the next generation's nineteen-births-ten-survivors ratio (§6.6), grim as it reads, was a ghetto-norm outcome rather than a singular family tragedy **[S]**. The surviving-children formula ("five of eight") recurs in the next generation with eerie symmetry (five of nineteen).
- Berghoeffer records, from Salomon Jakob Cohen's 1813 necrology, the community's own memory of the household: the father had intended his brilliant son for the rabbinate; the Memorbuch records no rabbi in the family (the rabbinic line belonged to a different branch — see §4.4); the family was pious and poor enough that Mayer's school years were short and hard **[A]**.

### 5.5 The brother the record kept: Moses Amschel (1736–1794)

Mayer's elder brother **Moses Amschel** ran the family money-changing business with him in the 1760s and was, in 1764–65, reportedly the **first Jew officially appointed exchange broker in Frankfurt** — a crack in the guild wall that mattered economically (legal recognition of Jewish brokerage) and symbolically **[B: Kober/juedischegeschichte.de]**. Moses Amschel married **Sarchen Lechnich** — a *second* Lechnich marriage, likely a cousin arrangement within the Hufeisen family network (one of our small unresolvable details: whether Sarchen was the sister or cousin of her mother-in-law Schönche). Their daughter **Edel (1777–1834)** married **Jacob David Goldschmidt**, and their son **Moritz von Goldschmidt-Rothschild (1803–1888)** — ennobled, Prussian consul in Vienna — would become a **co-founder of the Vienna house of Rothschild** in 1855. The female-line door that Mayer's will tried to close did not fully hold even in his lifetime's horizon: the Goldschmidt-Rothschilds carried the name and the blood into the third generation **[S/B]**. We flag this here because later chapters of the larger story must account for it: the "daughters excluded" rule bent at its first serious test.

## PART 6 — MAYER AMSCHEL ROTHSCHILD (1744–1812): THE MAKING OF THE FOUNDER

### 6.1 Birth: the 1743/1744 split, documented honestly

**Mayer Amschel Rothschild was born on 23 February 1744** — or possibly 1743 — in the Hinterpfann, Judengasse 188, Frankfurt **[S/A]**.

This is Anomaly 12, and it deserves careful treatment because both dates are *respectably* sourced:

- **1744**: Berghoeffer (1925, family archivist, Memorbuch fol. 51f.); the *Neue Deutsche Biographie* (2005); Amos Elon's *Founder* (1996); the *Encyclopaedia Judaica* (1972); the Rothschild Archive's own timeline; the standard English Wikipedia.
- **1743**: the *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* (1889); **Corti (1928)** — "born in the year 1743, six years before Goethe" (Goethe was born in 1749); the Frankfurter Personenlexikon, the city's official biographical dictionary, prints **"*1743 (oder 1744)"** — 1743, or 1744.

**Status: documented scholarly split.** The weight of modern scholarship and the family archive is 1744; the nineteenth-century tradition and Corti say 1743. No one has published the underlying Memorbuch entry's Hebrew date in the open literature to settle it. We therefore give **23 February 1744** as the working date with the split on record. The Frankfurter Personenlexikon's embarrassed double-entry is itself instructive: even the city's best dictionary stands on both sides of the seam.

His full Hebrew name, preserved on his tombstone, was **"Moshe Meier"** — and the stone styles him **"the Raw and Rabbi Moshe Meier"** — *Raw* (rabbi) and *Rabbi* — indicating that somewhere along the way he received rabbinic ordination, or at least the title **[A: Streun, transcribing the rediscovered stone; epidat database]**. The community-raised boy kept the scholars' world inside him all his life: the family firm corresponded in **Judeo-German written in Hebrew characters** into the 1850s, and the founder's grandchildren remembered Hebrew newspapers stacked under prayer books in the Green Shield **[A: Fraiman]**.

### 6.2 Childhood in the ghetto — what the walls actually were

To understand the childhood, you must understand the box it happened in. Frankfurt's ghetto — created in **1462**, thirty years after the plan of 1432, Europe's first walled Jewish quarter (Venice's came in 1516) — was a single lane, about **330 meters long**, built against the old city wall in the city's most polluted corner, the fish-market and tannery district **[S/B: Kracauer; METAhub]**. Its gates were locked at night, on Sundays, and through Good Friday to Easter. Its Jews could not own land, could not farm, were pushed out of most trades, could not walk through certain squares, had to step into the gutter and remove their hats on command — the street insult was "**Jud, mach Mores!**" ("Jew, do respect!") and it was ritualized; a *Judensau* (a degrading medieval sculpture of Jews suckling a sow) stood at the south bridgehead, still standing in Mayer's childhood **[S: Elon, ch. 1; B: Kriegk — who records that young Amschel himself endured the hat-ducking]**.

Some textures from Elon's chapter on the ghetto, all worth preserving because they are the ground truth of the dynasty's founding soil **[S]**:

- Jews paid a toll at the Fahrtor gate: Christians paid 4 kreuzer; "**Jews and oxen, 8**."
- After the great fire of 1711 the lane was widened to about 20 feet and houses were required to be three stories — that is why the Hinterpfann, though a back-house, had three floors.
- The ghetto burned in **1711, 1719, 1721, 1774, and 1796**.
- The community ran four synagogues, a bathhouse, a bakery famous for *shalet* (the Sabbath stew), a clinic — a complete civilization compressed into one street.
- Marriage was rationed: the *Judenstättigkeit* of 1616 (still in force) capped the community at **500 families** and allowed **twelve weddings a year**, grooms at 25 or older. Mayer's 1770 marriage squeaked through under these rules.
- In **1549**, Emperor Charles V had mortgaged the Jews of Frankfurt to the city for **15,200 pfund heller** — a loan against a people — and the massacres of 1241 and 1349 had repeatedly reduced the community. When Mayer was born, roughly **3,000–4,000 Jews** — about a tenth of Frankfurt — lived in the lane, in what the *Encyclopaedia Judaica* calls, citing the ghetto's own bitter nickname, "**New Egypt**."
- **1685:** Emperor **Leopold I renounced** the city's medieval right to "redeem" — buy out and expel — its Jews; the mortgage of 1549 had already converted a community into collateral, and this renunciation closed one legal trap while leaving every other rule standing **[S: Elon]**.
- **Visibility was taxed:** until 1726, Jews wore **two yellow rings sewn to their clothing**; women a **blue-striped veil** — identification as dress code **[B: Streun]**.
- **The street rules, completed:** selling at market only **after** Christians had finished selling; the lane closed entirely for the **Corpus Christi procession**; the **two-abreast** limit on walks; a **back entrance to the town hall** for Jewish petitioners (they did not use the front door); peddling fines outside the fair weeks; guild resistance keeping the permitted trade list (jewels, silk, lace) closed for generations **[S: Elon]**.
- **The law above the lane:** the community fought its battles at the imperial level — litigation before the **Reichshofrat in Vienna and the Imperial Chamber Court** through 1765–90, the files ("Juden wider Juden," preserved at the Frankfurt Jewish Community archive) forming the institutional backdrop to Mayer's generation's standing **[A/S — research lead]**.

Two scenes from the record capture the psychology this produced. In 1775, Moses Mendelssohn — the philosopher of the Berlin Enlightenment, passing through — was detained at the Fahrtor until a Jewish communal official vouched for him **[S: Elon]**. And half a century later, in 1833, Hans Christian Andersen visited the lane and noted, in his diary, that it was dark but not dirty — and that footmen in livery stood at **Gutle Rothschild's door** **[B: Fraiman, citing HCA's diary]**. The lane was poor; the family at No. 148 was not, and never apologized for it.

### 6.3 Orphanhood and the two educations

- **6 October 1755:** father dies of smallpox. **29 June 1756:** mother dies. Mayer is twelve, the fourth of eight children, the family's clever son **[A: Berghoeffer; G: encyclopedia.com]**. Corti's single line — "in 1755, in his twelfth year, he lost his father and mother" — compresses the nine months into one blow **[B]**; the records give the two dates, and we keep both.
- **The Fürth yeshiva (c. 1755–1757/63).** Mayer was sent to the Talmud school of **David Strauss in Fürth**, in Bavaria — the great Ashkenazi academy town. Why Fürth? The standard tellings say his father destined him for the rabbinate; the NDB explicitly doubts this, suggesting instead the aim was "merely the expansion of his religious and Hebrew knowledge," and the Frankfurt Memorbuch, the community's book of record, "is also silent" on any rabbinic ambition for Mayer **[S/A]**. We transmit the doubt rather than resolve it. What is certain: Fürth sits at the center of this family's intellectual geography. It was the publishing town of the rabbinic branch's **Boas Raphael Rothschild** (1766); it was home to the scholarly **Wetzlar** family (Solomon b. Simeon Wetzlar published *Ḥakirot ha-Lev* in Amsterdam in 1731 from Fürth). The boy was sent to the family's scholarly axis — whether to become a scholar or merely to be finished as a learned merchant, the sources genuinely differ, and we say so.

Berghoeffer adds one small hinge: the orphaned boy may have left Fürth *because the household collapsed* — the rabbinic track closing with the parents' deaths rather than with a decision **[A]**. And a detail of the ghetto's curriculum worth preserving: Torah study, yes — but **no Kabbalah before age forty**, by community rule; even the founder's education had a ceiling **[B: Streun]**.
- **The Oppenheimer apprenticeship (from c. 1757).** With his parents dead, Mayer was sent to **Hanover**, to the banking house of **Wolf Jakob Oppenheim** — the firm founded by **Simon Wolf Oppenheimer** and continued by his son Jacob Wolf, the grandson-in-law line of **Samuel Oppenheimer of Vienna** (1640–1703), the greatest imperial court Jew of the previous century **[S: Balliol College, Oxford, paper on Mendelssohn and the Ashkenazi court Jews; A: Rothschild Archive]**. What did he learn there? A later numismatic account describes the curriculum precisely: "**all dealings and businesses a court factor would do**" — bills of exchange, the discounting of princely paper, foreign trade finance, the arithmetic of many currencies **[B: CoinsWeekly, citing the literature]**.

  Here we must also update one of our own earlier findings. The Rothschild Archive states that **"family connections helped secure"** the apprenticeship **[A]** — which partially validates the claim in one popular biography (Streun) that Wolf Jakob was Mayer's "**uncle**." No kinship has been documented by name (we checked the Oppenheimer genealogy as far as public sources go: the apprenticeship line descends from Samuel Oppenheimer via Simon Wolf; the documented Rothschild-Oppenheim marriages — Kele bat Mendl Oppenheim of Worms, m. 1608; Reizche Rothschild m. Todros Oppenheim, 17th c. — run through the Worms/Frankfurt Oppenheims, whose exact relation to the Hanover line is not published). **Status: a connection existed per the family archive; its precise nature is unpublished. The "uncle" claim is unverified.** It is worth adding that the Archive simultaneously claims the Oppenheimers "branched out from the Frankfurt Judengasse to Vienna" — which conflicts with Stern's research placing Samuel Oppenheimer's line in Heidelberg/Worms. Even archives embed simplifications; we note it and move on.

  One date conflict on the apprenticeship itself: most authorities (and the Archive timeline) say **1757**; one genealogy site (Loeb) says "from 1765." **Resolved: c.1757**, with the firm unnamed in the earliest sources — the community's 1813 necrologist (Cohen) knew only "einige Jahre in einem jüdischen Handlungshaus zu Hannover" ("some years in a Jewish commercial house in Hanover"); Kriegk (1869) first named Oppenheim. The firm's identity is secure; the precise years are not.

- **Return (1764) and the first princely contact.** Mayer came home to Frankfurt, joined his brother Moses Amschel's money-changing business, and became a **dealer in rare coins, medals, and antiquities** — the collecting instinct of a boy who had handled coins at ten, now a trade. The gateway to princes was deliberate: coin-collecting was aristocratic fashion, and a coin dealer had legitimate reasons to wait on crown princes **[B: CoinsWeekly; S: EJ2]**. The documented milestones:
  - **From 1763–64:** General **Emmerich O. A. von Estorff** (1722–1796), a coin enthusiast attached to the Hessian court, became his patron and customer; Estorff later told the crown prince about "a young Jew named Rothschild who had bought coins for him in Hanover" **[B: Corti]**. The famous (and possibly embroidered) chess anecdote — the prince watching Mayer hold his own at the board and exclaiming, "Herr General, you have not recommended me a stupid man!" — comes from Kriegk's 1869 account; we grade it **tradition **[L]** with a documented core (the Estorff connection itself is solid).
  - **EJ2 dates the beginning of business with the future Landgrave William IX to c.1764**; Berghoeffer found, in the prince's household accounts, a **June 1765 payment of 38 gulden 30 kreutzer to "a Jew named Meyer" for medals** — his inference, flagged as inference, that this is our Mayer Amschel **[A/B]**.
  - **1778:** first entry in the Frankfurt address book: "antiques, medals and curiosities" **[A: Berghoeffer]**.
  - **Ten of his coin-sale catalogues survive** (in the library of Mayer Carl von Rothschild): six from the 1770s with sales of 1,666–2,917 gulden; four from the 1780s at 2,579–5,074 gulden — a business roughly doubling through the decade **[A: Berghoefter]**. The catalogues went to **Duke Karl August of Weimar** (Goethe's patron), the **Elector Palatine Karl Theodor**, and Prince William himself — a mail-order model, before the word existed: catalogues were posted, buyers marked selections, goods were sent on approval, the rest returned **[B: CoinsWeekly; Berghoeffer]**. A competitor's price guide (*Madai's Talerkabinett*) was his reference work; auctions at the Frankfurt fairs were his market floor.
  - **1796 texture:** his servant **Hirsch Liebmann** was arrested for stealing over 200 gold and silver medals — an accidental inventory proving the stock's size **[A: Berghoefter]**.

### 6.4 The court-Jew ladder: what "Hof-Faktor" actually meant

Mayer Amschel spent his life climbing a ladder of German-Jewish court titles, and each rung needs a plain explanation, because the system itself is the context of everything:

- **Court Jew (Hofjude):** in the fragmented Holy Roman Empire, princes needed bankers who could move money across borders, discount bills, arrange loans in Amsterdam and London, and supply armies — services Christian guilds and banks often would not or could not perform for small courts. Jews, excluded from land and guilds, built exactly this expertise. The position made a man rich and perpetually vulnerable: his protection was personal, not legal **[S: Selma Stern, *The Court Jew*]**. The classic example of the system's danger is Joseph Süß Oppenheimer (executed 1738) — whom, as we saw, **Moses Kalman Rothschild was doing documented business with in 1733–35**.
- **Hof-Faktor (court factor), 21 September 1769:** Mayer petitioned the Hessian court in a supplication letter that survives in full — "I have had the particular and high favor of making various deliveries to Your Serene Highness... [and] beg most humbly... that Your Highness may graciously grant me the title of **Fürstlich Hessen-Hanauischer Hof-Faktor**... which would be of great assistance to me in my trade and in other respects, to make my fortune here in the city of Frankfurt" **[A: Berghoeffer, quoting the document]**. The title was granted on 21 September 1769 — and the honor was physical: in golden letters under the Hesse-Hanau arms above his door at the Hinterpfann, the legend ran **"M.A. Rothschild, Hoflieferant Seiner Erlauchten Hoheit, Erbprinz Wilhelm von Hessen, Graf von Hanau"** — "M.A. Rothschild, purveyor to His Illustrious Highness, Hereditary Prince Wilhelm of Hesse, Count of Hanau" **[A: Streun, transcribing]**. A *Hof-Faktor* was a licensed purveyor to the court: the title did not yet make him the prince's banker; it made him his supplier, with the banker's door held open.
- **Hofagent (court agent), 1801:** the next rung — a financial agent of the prince, handling money, not just goods **[S: ADB]**.
- **Oberhofagent (chief court agent), 1803:** the documented summit of his Hessian career **[S: German Wikipedia, citing Pohl]**.
- **Kaiserlicher Hof-Faktor (imperial court factor):** the Frankfurter Personenlexikon — the city's official dictionary — states that **from 1792 Mayer supplied the Imperial (Habsburg) armies** and that this earned him appointment as **imperial court factor**; the Rothschild Archive's timeline formalizes the title in **1800** ("Imperial Crown Agent") **[B/A]**. No other authority mentions this title, so we record it as: claimed by the city lexicon and the family archive in substance (imperial army contracts from 1792, title by 1800), uncorroborated elsewhere, and chronologically sensible — 1792–97 was precisely when the Frankfurt houses fed the Austrian war effort. Two further titles in the same lexicon entry: **court factor of the Order of St John (Johanniterorden)** and **of the Princes of Thurn und Taxis** (the latter the hereditary postmasters of the Empire — a natural client for a man whose advantage was *moving value*).
- **Posthumous honors:** Austria raised the **five sons** to hereditary nobility (1816/17 — "von Rothschild"); the 1817 imperial grant is sometimes described (loosely) as raising Mayer himself posthumously; the baronial rank with the famous five-arrow arms followed in **1822**, with the motto *Concordia, Integritas, Industria* and — the detail that matters most — a **red shield** at the heart of the coat of arms **[A: Rothschild Archive]**. The full blazon: on the breast of the double eagle, a **red heart-shield bearing a silver target** (the house-sign, quartered into nobility), the **five arrows held in a fist** (the motif the Hanadiv Foundation still carries as its emblem), with the lion and unicorn as supporters; the hereditary nobility reached the five brothers in **1816/17**, and the baronial patent itself is dated **29 September 1822** **[A/B]**. The family ennobled itself into its own house-sign.

**The predecessor who taught the market.** Two documented men held the position Mayer would ultimately take, and both matter. **Veidel (Feidel) David** was the Landgrave's principal court agent at Kassel when Mayer was a small supplier; Corti records that as late as 1789 a single transaction **thirty times the size** of Rothschild's was entrusted to Veidel David, while noting that the Rothschilds' later rise "was facilitated" by David's having pioneered the very transactions they would perfect — a scholarly article in *Aschkenas* (2011) is devoted to precisely this: "**Hofjude ohne Hof: the Kassel chief court agent Feidel David, a predecessor of Mayer Amschel Rothschild**" **[S/B]**. The rival house of **Breidenbach** (Wolf Breidenbach, court factor to the Elector of Hesse) was the standing competition at Wilhelm's court **[S: court-Jew scholarship]**. Mayer's achievement was not inventing the role — it was inheriting a mature, dangerous profession and executing it at a scale and with a security architecture no predecessor matched.

### 6.5 William of Hesse: the client of a lifetime

The prince at the center of the story — **Wilhelm IX of Hesse-Kassel** (born 3 June 1743, Landgrave from 1785, Elector from 1803; the richest prince in Germany and, arguably, the largest private lender in Europe — his grandfather Frederick II had famously sold 12,000 Hessian soldiers to Britain, at a net profit estimated around 3.5 million marks, which became the fortune Mayer administered) — enters the documented record of the family through coins, and stays for everything else **[S/B: Corti; Berghoeffer]**. His world, fixed by Corti from the state papers: grandfather **William VIII died in 1760**; Frederick II's Catholicism was secret until the *Religionsassekurationsakte* of 1754; during the prince's minority the regency of **Maria of England (1760–64)** governed; William entered his Hanau inheritance on **22 October 1764** — the year Mayer turned twenty; Hanau held 50,000 inhabitants; the prince was a **Freemason** (a fact the lodge-lore literature later inflates); his morganatic favorites (the Haynau, Heimrod, Hessenstein families) received titles from the Emperor **in return for loans** — the era's credit-for-honors market in miniature; he sold the small Hanau regiment to England in **1776**; and before the Rothschilds took the channel, his English fortune was managed through the **Amsterdam house Van der Noten** **[B: Corti]**. Key beats:

- **1785:** Frederick II dies (31 October 1785); Wilhelm inherits, debt-free, one of Europe's great fortunes.
- **1787:** Mayer attends the Cassel court with coins and jeweled gold chains priced deliberately low — a loss-leader; two more years pass before serious business follows **[B: Corti]**.
- **1789:** Mayer's surviving petition letter about bills of exchange; Wilhelm's inquiries return the verdict that would define the relationship: "**punctual in payments, energetic and honorable**" **[A/B: Corti, from the Buderus papers]**.
- **From 1790:** the family's decisive document — **Carl Friedrich Buderus**. Buderus was the Landgrave's asset manager: the son of a Hanau schoolmaster who had been writing-and-music master to the children of the crown prince's mistress (an ancestress of the Haynau family), a man who rose by fiscal genius — his milk-fraction scheme adding 120 thalers here, a salt tax there, officials paid on percentage **[B: Corti]**. The archive-cited mechanism of the introduction: **Buderus had business dealings with Wolf Salomon Schnapper (Mayer's father-in-law) as early as 1790, and it was Schnapper who brought Buderus and Mayer Amschel together** **[B: Corti, Buderus papers]** — the small-bank court agent handing his son-in-law the key to the treasury.
- **1794–98:** the first great test — moving the Landgrave's English investments home through the war years; Buderus proposed Rothschild alongside Bethmann and four other firms for a share; Wilhelm chose **Bethmann** alone. Corti's verdict from the papers: Rothschild was left out — "**but it did not happen again**" **[B]**.
- **1806–07:** Napoleon dissolves the Holy Roman Empire; Wilhelm flees to exile (his second, longer exile, 1806–13, spent between his Baltic estates and Prague). The fortune — **£600,000 in English stock** plus chests of documents — did not follow him into the open; it stayed in Mayer's hands and his sons' networks, and the servicing of it (at interest, scrupulously accounted) became the family's banking apprenticeship. The *Jewish Encyclopedia* details the mechanics: the elector's English deposits held for him, interest paid; gold shipments for Wellington's army moving through the same channels; **four simultaneous profit streams** on the elector's money **[S]**.
- The elector died in 1821, still the family's foundational client; his business had trained the five brothers in everything: state loans, bullion movement, exchange operations, and the legal shadow-world of wartime finance.

### 6.6 Marriage: Gutle Schnapper (1753–1849)

**The wedding: 29 August 1770.** Mayer married **Gutle (Gudula) Schnapper**, seventeen years old, eldest child of **Wolf Salomon Schnapper** (1728–1811) — and here we correct the record of even the Rothschild Archive, which calls her "daughter of Baruch Schnapper, a Frankfurt tradesman." The documented name is **Wolf Salomon Schnapper**, with **Baruch** as a second given name (crowdsourced records render him "Wolf Salomon Baruch Schnapper"; the Archive evidently used the second name). He lived at the house **"zur Eule"** (at the owl) and ran a **small bank**; he was **court agent to the Duke of Saxe-Meiningen** — granted, in June 1789, the precious ghetto-exit pass allowing Sunday and holiday business travel for the Duke's affairs **[A/B: Berghoeffer; Fraiman, citing Dietz; METAhub family history]**. He died on **21 November 1811**, having raised **seven children**; four siblings are recorded for him, including a Geliche — the Eule household was large **[B/C]**. Gutle was born "**probably at the Eule**" (METAhub), and her parents' marriage is dated **1752** — the MyHeritage calculation "at age 29" fits Bella's birth of ~1723 **[C→B]**.

**Her mother's side.** Gutle's mother was **Bella Bejle Gans** (b. ~1723, d. **1759** per Dietz's *Stammbuch*, resolving a 1757/1759 conflict in favor of 1759), daughter of **Abraham Mayer Gans "zur Trommel"** (at the drum; d. 1786) and **Frumet Schnapper** (d. 1793) — note the doubled Schnapper blood on both sides, an endogamy pattern the family would later elevate to doctrine. Crowdsourced trees variously render Bella as "Trier Gans" or "Bikkert zu Trier"; neither variant is verifiable in any scholarly source (German-Jewish surname scholarship documents *Bickert* as a real surname, but no Frankfurt-Trier link), and we record both as garble **[C — flagged]**. The wider Gans family was one of German Jewry's old name-sign houses ("Gans" = goose); a branch of the Gans family had intermarried with the great Hanover court-Jew network of Glückel of Hameln's world (her daughter Jente married Salomon Gans, then the great court financier **Leffmann Behrens**) — the same Behrens-Oppenheimer web that trained Mayer **[B: Danish genealogical literature]**. And one elegant coincidence worth preserving: the **Trier** family held the family's ancestral house in 1715 — "1715: Gumprecht Trier im roten Schild" appears in the ghetto register quoted by the ADB — so the house-sign thread touches both sides of Gutle's ancestry **[S: ADB]**.

**The marriage terms.** Dowry: **2,400 gulden** — approximately Mayer's annual income at the time; a marriage of equals in the ghetto's ledger-books **[S/A: EJ2; Fraiman]**. The *Judenstättigkeit* rules (groom 25+) were satisfied: he was 26. It was an arranged marriage — the norm — and by every surviving account, an exceptionally good one. Gutle gave birth **nineteen times** between 1771 and 1792; **ten children survived** — five sons, five daughters **[S: Ferguson, vol. 1 ("nineteen or so children, ten lived"); Fraiman, citing Miriam Rothschild's *The Rothschilds: A Family Portrait*; Livingstone 2022]**. (The Frankfurter Personenlexikon says "twenty children"; we weight nineteen, the figure in the family-centered literature, and note the outlier — see the Register, #17.)

**She was a banker.** The 2022 history *The Women of Rothschild* (Natalie Livingstone) documents what the archive letters had always implied: Gutle **cashed and issued bills** and managed the household's finances; Mayer discussed business with her; when he traveled, Frankfurt ran through her **[B/S]**. The family's own sexism is equally documented — her son Carl wrote to Salomon complaining about women as "**bad cashiers**" — which makes her documented competence the more telling. She never left the Judengasse. Not after the ghetto walls legally fell, not after she was one of the wealthiest women in Europe, not after her sons built palaces in five capitals. Hans Christian Andersen saw her footmen in livery at the ghetto door in 1833; the diarist Charles Greville watched her, well-dressed, driving to the opera in a small luxurious carriage past "wretched, gloomy, dilapidated" surroundings in June 1843 **[B: Fraiman, citing both diaries]**. She outlived her husband by thirty-seven years, dying on **7 May 1849** — two months after the revolutionary parliament's law of 20 February 1849 finally granted Frankfurt's Jews full equality, her sons beside her; her **bridal wreath was preserved under glass** in the family house (Corti's detail). She is buried in the **Rat-Beil-Strasse** cemetery — the new one, the old Battonnstraße ground having closed in 1828. The Archiv's terse story about her is worth quoting: asked by a French investigator what she did, she answered that she concerned herself only with her housework. The investigator believed her. The bills in her hand said otherwise **[B: Fraiman]**.

**Her portrait.** Moritz Daniel Oppenheim — the "painter of the Rothschilds," who made some forty portraits of the family — painted Gutle from life, signed **1840**, 78 × 65 cm, with two preparatory sketches now in the Israel Museum. The painting was **lost in the Second World War**; it survives through a glass negative in the Jewish Museum Frankfurt's archives and the sketches. The portrait's background is quietly falsified, and the falsification is the point: the window shows the synagogue square as recorded by Reiffenstein in 1845 — a view the Green Shield never had — Oppenheim seating the matriarch inside her ancestral street **[B: Fraiman, full article captured]**. Earlier portraits (an 1836 Eckhardt) exist in two versions.

### 6.7 The House at the Green Shield — the Stammhaus

- **Purchased in two halves, 1783 and 1785** (Berghoeffer, from the city archives); a Hebrew purchase contract survives, with a **Sukkah clause** — the right to build the harvest booth; possession and the family's move followed in **1786** **[A: Berghoefter; B: Fraiman]**. Popular sources (ADB 1889; Kriegk 1869; METAhub) say 1780 or 1785; the Archive timeline says 1785; we weight the archival two-tranche account (1783/85, moved 1786) and log the cluster. For three years after the first purchase the family rented another Judengasse home.
- The house: **No. 148**, built 1615, a **4.30-meter-wide** facade (one source: 4.70 m), eleven and a half meters deep, four stories — one of the finest addresses in the ghetto, opposite the middle western gate. The family lore: all the children shared **one bedroom**; the kitchen, twelve by five feet, had a **one-pot hearth**; the famous back room, **nine feet by nine**, was the **first banking house** — counting table, a large iron chest that opened from the back, and **secret wall-shelves** behind panels; a rooftop terrace for the Sukkah with a view of the lane; their own water pump — a ghetto luxury **[S/B: Elon; Corti; Fraiman]**. Mayer also acquired the adjacent **Golden Ark** (Goldene Arche) house.
- The family kept the Green Shield as its symbolic home for generations after it ceased to be a residence. It survived the ghetto's nineteenth-century demolitions (the lane was razed in 1840 and 1884–85; renamed Börnestrasse after Ludwig Börne, the baptized son of the ghetto, born thirty doors away in 1786) and became a museum. **Destroyed by bombs on 18 May 1944** — Streun's account, correcting the common claim that the Stammhaus "still survives." What survives is the archaeological site: during construction at Battonnstraße 47 in 1987, remains of five ghetto houses were excavated, and the **Museum Judengasse** was built over them **[B: Streun; METAhub]**. The family's 1886-rebuilt Stammhaus (after the city expropriated the plot in 1882 for 15,250 marks, rebuilt by architect Franz von Hoven in 1886 at Börnestrasse 26, housing the family's foundations and a Gumpertz Zuntz apartment) is the structure that the 1944 bombs destroyed. The legal chain — 1872 Oppenheim's widow vacated; 1882 expropriation; 1885 purchase contract for 35,460 marks; 1886 reopening under Amschel Mayer's testamentary foundation (1.2 million gulden for poor Israelites of the community, plus a 50,000-gulden dowry fund) — is fully documented in Berghoeffer's first chapter **[A]**.
- The **1796 bombardment** (Kléber's artillery, 12–14 July 1796) destroyed half the ghetto and broke the walls in practice: some 2,000 Jews, the Rothschilds among them, camped outside the lane; Mayer rented business premises **outside the ghetto for the first time** — the first legal breach of the walls the family ever made **[G/B]**. From 1796 the firm kept offices in the **Schnurgasse** (outside the lane) with warehouse space at the **Trierischer Hof**; in **1809** Mayer bought the burned building plot at the Fahrgasse corner in the ghetto's northern end, where the later bank house at **Fahrgasse 146** rose — the corner house from which the five brothers ran the family bank from 1813 **[B: Frankfurter Personenlexikon; Streun — who records that Mayer ordered the new bank building's construction the day before he died]**.

## PART 7 — THE BUSINESS: WHAT MAYER ACTUALLY DID, 1770–1812

### 7.1 The goods trade — the forgotten foundation

The popular image of Mayer Amschel is of a coin dealer who became a banker. The documentary reality, excavated by Berghoeffer from the Frankfurt city archives and the Carlshausen (Buderus) papers, is that **for most of his career Mayer was a merchant — and the goods trade, not banking, was the business's foundation until about 1810** **[A]**. Berghoeffer's thesis, in his own words: like Metzler, Bethmann (who began as the Adami firm's heir), Hollweg, Grunelius and the other great Frankfurt houses, **the bank grew out of the trading house**. The documented file:

- **1796:** suppliers **Bradforth of Yorkshire** and **Sisson Wood & Co. of Leeds** (English cloth); warehouse rent 400 gulden 24 kreuzer a year **[A]**.
- **1802:** **Konrad Knopf of Nuremberg** (batiste, muslin).
- **July 1806:** **Starman & Co., London** — 14 bales of coffee, 6 chests plus 3 seroons of indigo, shipped on the *Mercurius*, Captain Jacobs, to Hamburg **[A]**.
- **1807:** Hamburg cotton purchases; **1809:** sugar and tobacco; **1810:** cloth, hare skins, sheep's wool, arrack, wine; Macedonian cotton; Prague houses among the correspondents. England was the main source — which is why **Nathan's 1798 dispatch to Manchester** (to buy English goods at source) is the true beginning of the family's international structure **[A/B]**.
- The **1809 police interrogation** (during the Continental System crackdown) records Mayer describing his business as exchange *and* goods trading, with **four sons active in it** **[A]**.
- The goods ledger's texture: in 1809–10 the firm shipped **£6,023 worth of cambrics** to Amsterdam for "James Mayer"; 900 dozen cloths bought for 9,700 gulden returned a profit of 2,229 gulden — a **23% margin**; indigo lots from Echenique & Co.; insurance running at **35–50% of cargo value** — the wartime risk premium on every shipment **[A]**.
- **The Continental System** (Napoleon's embargo on British goods, 1806–10) hit Frankfurt — Europe's entrepôt — like a siege: the Trianon tariff of August 1810 imposed 40–50% duties; the Fontainebleau decrees of October 1810 ordered sequestration and public burning; **234 Frankfurt firms were ruined** in what Berghoeffer calls "the plundering of the Frankfurt merchant class"; the Rothschilds' own declared English goods — **60,000 gulden, of which 27,000 was indigo** — were caught in the seizures **[A]**. The goods business was wound down; what remained in the 1810 partnership deed's list of assets was bills, bonds, state paper, wine, and cash: **the firm had become, de facto, a bank**.

### 7.2 Bills, the elector, and the war-economy apprenticeship

From the 1790s, the bill-of-exchange business (discounting princely and commercial paper) grew alongside goods. Under the Continental System and the wars, Frankfurt became the hub of **smuggling finance** — moving British goods and British gold into the Continent — and the Rothschilds were documented participants: their couriers ran under **code names** ("Herr von Goldstein," "Johannes Adler," "der Principale"); the firm kept **double books**; and in 1810 the French authorities confiscated goods and burned records (one documented loss: 60,000 gulden in paper, plus a 20,000-franc fine) **[A: German Wikipedia's detailed article, citing Elon and Ferguson]**.

The structure of the Napoleonic era, in the archive's own summary: **1798 — "the first Rothschild family partnership"** (the arrangement dispatching Nathan to England — the Archive counts *this*, not the 1810 contract, as the family's first partnership); **1806 — "the first Rothschild client: the Elector of Hesse-Kassel"** in the formal banking sense; **1810 — the partnership contract of 27 September** ("Mayer Amschel Rothschild & Söhne," original in the Frankfurt city archive), reorganizing the firm among the five sons with the father holding the decisive voice; Nathan, who had declined a Frankfurt partnership share in **1807**, accepted in **1809** — the London connection thus became contractual precisely as the elector's English fortune needed servicing. The documented volumes: by 1813, **nine transactions totaling £664,850** moved through the brothers' network for the elector, at a commission of **⅛%** on bill transactions **[A/G]**. The famous smuggling coup of the war years — the four parallel profit streams on the elector's money (documented by the *Jewish Encyclopedia* from the family papers): holding his English deposits and paying him interest; profiting on the exchange operations; earning commissions; and placing the funds in their own expanding loan business **[S]**. This is how a court supplier became a state banker: not by a single legendary coup, but by seven years of punctual, energetic, honorable paper-pushing — the elector's own verdict on Mayer, preserved in the Buderus files.

### 7.3 The brothers take their posts

- **Nathan Mayer (1777–1836)** — third son. Manchester from 1798/99 with about **£20,000** of the family's capital; his own account, given to the abolitionist Sir Thomas Buxton in 1834 and preserved by the *Jewish Encyclopedia*: he began with £20,000, "trebled" it in about seven years by vertical integration (raw material + dye + manufacture: "What another can do, I can do also"), and moved to London in **1808**, acquiring the lease of **No. 2 New Court, St Swithin's Lane, in 1809 for £750** — the address of N M Rothschild & Sons to this day; the Manchester operation was wound up by 1811. He married **Hannah Barent-Cohen** — sister-in-law of Moses Montefiore — binding the family to Anglo-Jewry's grandest network **[S/A]**. A detail with a long afterlife in the lore literature: Nathan was **initiated as a Freemason** (Emulation Lodge No. 21, London, 24 October 1802) — a documented fact about one man that conspiracy literature later inflates into a family-wide secret **[A: masonic records; X-tier inflation]**. He died **in Frankfurt, 28 July 1836**, during a family meeting — the *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* preserves the detail **[S]**.
- **Salomon Mayer (1774–1855)** — to Vienna; from 1815 the conduit for British subsidy payments to the Continent (via Herries); Vienna did not permit Jews to own houses, so he famously **rented an entire hotel**; founded what became the **Creditanstalt** (formally by his son Anselm in 1855) **[S]**.
- **Carl (Kalman) Mayer (1788–1855)** — courier of the network during the war years (at Dunkirk in the goods-trade file), then **Naples from 1821** **[S/A]**.
- **James (Jacob) Mayer (1792–1868)** — to Paris **1811/12**, secretly collecting French coin for Wellington's army before the end of the war; **De Rothschild Frères** formally founded 1817; the rue Laffitte salon that Heine, Balzac, Rossini, Chopin, Ingres and the Queen of the French all attended; died worth, by one estimate, 600 million francs — more than all other Paris bankers combined (that estimate is a contemporary's, and we tag it **[B]**).
- **Amschel Mayer (1773–1855)** — the eldest, stayed in Frankfurt at the Stammhaus; childless; the family's Frankfurt pillar, admitted with his brothers to the elite Casino society only in 1836 **[S/B]**.

### 7.4 Emancipation — bought, rescinded, and bought again

The ghetto's end came with the French: Napoleon made Frankfurt a Grand Duchy under **Karl von Dalberg** (1806), and in **1810** the ghetto was abolished. But Frankfurt's Jews did not walk free — they **bought** their equality: a ransom of **440,000 gulden**, of which the Rothschild contribution (440,000 as "the large partial sum" — effectively underwriting the community's payment) is documented; the emancipation edict followed on **7 February 1811**, with an annual "concessions money" still due **[S/G: German Wikipedia, citing Ferguson]**. Mayer was among the first Jews entered in the Frankfurt citizen-book in 1812 — and the equality was **revoked two years later** when the city regained self-government; full civic equality came only in **1864** (Frankfurt the second German city to grant it) — and, poignantly, the *third* and final emancipation arrived in law on 20 February 1849, ten weeks before Gutle's death **[S/B]**. The family's relationship to emancipation was therefore double from the start: it paid for it, profited from the partial versions Napoleon delivered, and lived long enough to see the final statute.

### 7.5 The will — the constitution of a dynasty

Mayer Amschel fell ill in 1808 and made a first will; on **17 September 1812**, before six witnesses, he made a new one; **the day before his death he ordered the construction of the new Frankfurt bank building** at the Fahrgasse corner; he died on **19 September 1812**, a Friday — his tombstone (as transcribed after its rediscovery) records that he died on the holy Sabbath's eve and was buried on Sunday, the eve of Sukkot, "of the year 5573 by the small reckoning" **[A: Streun, full inscription; the civil dates and the Hebrew inscription are internally consistent — verified by calendar arithmetic: 19 September 1812 was a Friday, 20 September the eve of Sukkot]**.

The will's documented provisions (transmitted by Berghoeffer, the *Jewish Encyclopedia*, and the German Wikipedia article):

1. **Secrecy as policy**: discretion enjoined; the children's first-name order permuted as a code; no inventory or publication of the wealth; correspondence in cipher and Judendeutsch.
2. **The core rule**: each child should leave the next generation **at least as much as he inherited**.
3. **The exclusion**: "I ordain and will that my daughters and sons-in-law and their heirs shall have **no share** in the existing firm 'Meyer Amschel Rothschild und Söhne'" **[A]** — the legal root of the famous **endogamy**: with daughters excluded from capital, cousin-marriage kept the money inside. The first such marriage was James to his niece Betty in 1824; by 1905, **29 of 58** recorded family marriages were between first cousins — a rate the *Jewish Encyclopedia* called, with Victorian bluntness, "anthropologically significant" **[S]**. (The rule bent in practice within one generation, via the Goldschmidt-Rothschild line of his brother — see §5.5.)
4. **The 70,000 gulden**: Gutle received 70,000 gulden plus the right of residence in the Green Shield for life **[B: Fraiman]**.
5. **The 5,000 gulden**: each son was to send 5,000 gulden yearly to the Frankfurt house for the poor — the dynasty's charitable tithe, recorded by both ADB and Kriegk **[S/B]**.
6. **Consequence**: the will's terms made the eventual 1901 liquidation of the Frankfurt house structurally inevitable once its male line failed — the firm could not pass through daughters **[A: Streun]**.

### 7.6 Burial and the stone that came back

Mayer was buried in the old Jewish cemetery on **Battonnstraße** — a ground first mentioned in 1180, holding some 20,000 burials until it closed in 1828; his stone was among the last placed there, at the cemetery's edge of fullness. In 1938 the Nazis tried to destroy it; the cemetery's 4,666 gravestones were demolished in 1942; 175 stones were saved, Mayer's among them; the epitaphs were documented in 1991–99 and published by the Steinheim Institute (**epidat** database); Mayer's stone was **rediscovered in the 1960s** and re-erected in an honored place — his grave is now part of the cemetery's Ehrenhain (grove of honor), and the city's Personenlexikon confirms the post-1945 rediscovery **[A/B: Streun; Steinheim/epidat; Frankfurter Personenlexikon]**. The full Hebrew inscription, as transcribed, styles him "the Raw and Rabbi Moshe Meier, son of the eternal and honored Amschel Rothschild..." — a rabbi's titles on a banker's stone, the family's two trades fused in two words.

**Wealth at death.** Berghoeffer's documented balance: in **1797** the firm's books showed **108,504 gulden**; by the 1800s the family stood among the **eleven richest families in the Judengasse** — one rich family among several on a poor street **[A: Berghoeffer; Ferguson via Streun]**. The princely scale came after: the *Jewish Encyclopedia* records the firm as **the richest in Europe by the end of 1818**, on Ehrenberg's analysis, largely through "bearing" operations on government loans — and Gentz's famous ledger (noted by the ADB) credits the Rothschild network with moving **1.1–1.2 billion gulden for European states in twelve years**: ~500 million for England, ~200 for France, ~120 each for Austria and Naples, ~100 for Prussia, 60 for Russia. Mayer himself never saw those numbers; his sons built them on the foundation of his one rule: punctual, energetic, honorable.

## PART 8 — THE NETWORK MAP: EVERY FAMILY THE ROTHSCHILDS MARRIED, DEALT WITH, OR DESCENDED FROM

Genealogy is usually drawn as a tree. The documented reality is a **web** — the Frankfurt ghetto's marriage market was a closed circuit of a few dozen families, and every name in this section appears in the record connected to the Rothschilds by either a marriage, a bill, or a house. We present each as what it is: documented connection, not adopted theory.

### 8.1 The Bacharach question
The family's reconstructed genealogies (and the rabbinic branch's own double signature — Mendel of Worms signing both "Rothschild" and "Bacharach") attach the line to the **Bacharach** rabbinical dynasty of Worms/Mainz (whose surname came from the town of Bacharach on the Rhine; the name appears in Worms 1449, Mainz 1455, Frankfurt 1516). The famous Bacharach rabbis — Avraham Shmuel Bacharach (1575–1616), his son Moshe Shimshon (1607–1670), his grandson **Yair Chayyim Bacharach** (1639–1702, author of *Havvot Yair*) — are documented. The dynasty's own texture, for the record: its matriarch **Chava** was a granddaughter of the **Maharal of Prague** and refused marriage to **Isaiah Horowitz (the Shelah)**; the family fled the 1615 Worms pogrom to Gernsheim, and Yair Chayyim died at Alsbach. This is the documented house the reconstruction invokes; the **bridge** between that dynasty and Isaac Elchanan's family is reconstruction only. The claim "Uri Feibesch **Bacharach**" appears only in crowdsourced trees **[C]**. The DNA question this raises is in Part 9. **Status: documented as tradition within the rabbinic branch itself; unproven as genealogy.**

### 8.2 Wetzlar
The **Wetzlar** family of Frankfurt: **R. Lejw Wetzlar**, rabbi and scholar, built the house "zum Bunten Löwen" in 1584 and died in 1609 — "a lion among his contemporaries"; the family's descendants include the apostate **Karl Abraham Wetzlar** (1714–1799), an Offenbach-born army contractor ennobled as **Wetzlar von Plankenstern** by Joseph II after his 1776 conversion (his wife Leonore refused baptism and died Jewish — the era's apostasy dynamic in one household) **[S: Jewish Encyclopedia/Encyclopaedia Judaica "Wetzlar"; B]**. Marriages into the Rothschild line: **Eva Wetzlar** m. Elchanan "Hähnchen" in **December 1585**; **Ella Wetzlar** m. the Moses Rothschild who died 1657 **[A: 1912 tree]**. Whether Eva and Ella belong to the documented Wetzlar-von-Plankenstern genealogy is unproven but chronologically and geographically plausible **[flagged: plausible, unbridged]**.

### 8.3 Oppenheim of Worms (and the Hanover bank)
Two Oppenheimer systems must be kept apart. (1) The **Worms/Frankfurt Oppenheims**: the 1608 marriage of **Kele bat Mendl Oppenheim of Worms** to Rabbi Isaac Rothschild; her possible kinship to **R. Mendel Oppenheim of Worms** (d. 1635, whose relatives donated 100 imperial taler to the cemetery fence, per the ShUM documentation) is a **lead, not a fact**. (2) The **Vienna/Hanover Oppenheimers**: Samuel Oppenheimer (1640–1703), imperial court Jew of Vienna; his grandson **Simon Wolf Oppenheimer** (d. Hanover 1726), who married Fradel Behrends, granddaughter of the great Hanover court factor Leffmann Behrens; their son **Jacob Wolf Oppenheimer** (d. 1760), in whose bank Mayer Amschel apprenticed from c.1757 **[S: Stern; Balliol paper; A: Archive]**. The Behrends intermarriages (and David Oppenheim, chief rabbi of Prague, who married Gnendl Behrends) tie this house to the wider court-Jew aristocracy; a branch — Frumet Oppenheimer's descendants — married into Moses Mendelssohn's family, producing Felix Mendelssohn **[B: genealogy literature]**.

### 8.4 Kaz / Kalonymos
**Kalonymos Kaz** — Naftali Hirz's father-in-law (marriage contract, 3 May 1634; his daughter Schönche's gravestone 879; Memorbuch fol. 40). The **Kalonymos** name is one of Ashkenazi Jewry's most illustrious (the medieval dynasty of Lucca→Mainz→Speyer, associated with the rise of Hasidei Ashkenaz); a Frankfurt family *Kaz/Kalonymos* intermarried with the Rothschilds, and the Avotaynu-published index of Frankfurt families (built on Ettlinger's transcription) independently records the pairing "Kalonymus-Kaz — Rothschild." Whether this Kaz line truly descends from the medieval Kalonymos cannot be proven from the name alone — scholars caution that the name's recurrence does not guarantee descent **[S: scholarship on the Kalonymos family; A: Ettlinger-based indices]**. Documented fact: a 1634 marriage into a family bearing one of European Jewry's greatest names.

### 8.5 Lechnich (Hufeisen)
**Meier Lechnich** — whose daughter Schönche married Amschel Moses — lived at the house **zum Hufeisen** (at the horseshoe; built 1561; 1.70-meter facade). Dietz's verdict on the family: **penniless** — secondhand-clothes dealers; by mid-century, Herts Lechnich had accumulated 5,000 gulden **[B: METAhub, citing Dietz]**. Two Lechnich marriages in two generations (Amschel Moses and his son Moses Amschel) — likely cousins, unresolvable in detail — make this the founder's maternal clan, the poor kin the dynasty never forgot: it was to such families that the will's poor-fund tithe flowed.

### 8.6 Waag, Buxbaum, Höchst

## PART 9 — THE DNA THREAD: WHAT GENETICS CAN AND CANNOT SAY

Since the 2000s, Y-chromosome testing has added a genuinely new evidence stream — one that must be handled with the same care as the documents, because hobbyist genetics has its own folklore.

**What is measured.** Y-DNA passes father-to-son, like a surname. The **Rothschild DNA Project** (FamilyTreeDNA) holds samples from documented male-line Rothschilds, placed in haplogroup **J2a1, subclade Y23457** (one sample: a Rothschild of Germany, d. 1781; another: a Rothschild b. 1822, tested to Big Y-500) **[C-grade project data]**. Haplogroup J2 is a lineage whose deepest origins lie in the Near East/West Asia — consistent with the documented paternal ancestry of Ashkenazi Jews, a population whose genome-wide ancestry is Levantine-Middle Eastern and Southern European in composition **[S: population genetics consensus]**.

**The Khazar test.** Antisemitic literature claims Ashkenazi Jews descend from the Khazar khaganate (a Turkic steppe empire of the early Middle Ages), and casts the Rothschilds accordingly — sometimes as "fake Jews" leading a hidden Khazar empire **[X]**. The genetic evidence at population level refutes this: the genome-wide study by Behar et al. (2013, *Human Biology*, 20+ co-authors) found "**no evidence... of a Khazar origin** for the Ashkenazi Jews," locating their ancestry instead in the Near East and Southern Europe **[S: PubMed 25079123]**. The Rothschild family's own J2 Y-line fits this pattern. (Population-level refutation does not, of course, adjudicate any individual family's medieval geography — but the specific "Khazar Rothschild" genealogy exists only in conspiracy literature, where it functions as a rebranded version of older "wandering Jew" tropes.)

**The Bacharach match — the open question.** The Bacharach Y-DNA project places documented Bacharach rabbinic lines in the same broad J2 neighborhood (subclades L556 / Y13373 per project phylogenies and the Avotaynu-published study "From Kansas to the Rhine," Paull & Bronstein). Eupedia's J2 reference page lists the Rothschilds and the Bacharachs together as exemplars of the cluster. **But the specific, genealogically-meaningful question — are the Frankfurt banking Rothschilds patrilineal descendants of the Bacharach dynasty? — is not publicly established**: a 2022 discussion among project genealogists records precisely this open question, and no peer-reviewed match within genealogical time has been published. Meanwhile, the *documentary* evidence (Mendel of Worms signing both names) shows the two names intertwined within the family a full century before DNA testing — which is evidence of *usage*, not of the deeper patrilineal claim. **Status: open; answerable only by targeted Big-Y testing of documented male-line Rothschild and Bacharach descendants.** We preserve the question rather than adopt the affirmative answer that hobbyist pages assume, or the negative that skeptics prefer.

## PART 10 — THE LORE REGISTRY: EVERY MYTH, ITS ORIGIN, AND ITS VERDICT

This section documents the myth apparatus in full — not because any of it is true, but because two centuries of it *are* part of the historical record, and because each myth, dissected, teaches how evidence actually works. Method for each: state the claim, trace its origin, render the verdict.

### 10.1 "Their real name was Bauer" — **[X] → resolved (Anomaly 11)**
**Claim:** the family was originally named Bauer and changed its name to Rothschild in 1744, concealing its identity **[X]**. **Origin:** the claim circulates endlessly in modern conspiracy media; a Chinese-language version repeats it verbatim, and an MA thesis has even cited it. **Verdict:** false as stated; but with a documented kernel stranger than the myth: Frankfurt registers record **Kalman Rothschild "genannt Bauer"** (d. 1707) and **Moses Kalman Rothschild "genannt Bauer"** (d. 1735) — the family was *called* Bauer in official records, almost certainly from the Bauer family co-residing in the Hinterpfann (Elon). Mayer's infant nickname was "das Bäuerchen." No name-change ever occurred: "Rothschild" descends from a house the family occupied from the 1560s and left in the seventeenth century.

### 10.2 The Waterloo legend — **[X] → debunked, with a documented genealogy**
**Claim:** Nathan Rothschild witnessed Waterloo (or rode from the battlefield), received the news first by pigeon, crashed the London market by pretending defeat, and made "£135 million" (or $500 billion in modern clickbait). **Origin, fully documented:** a literary seed in **Balzac's** 1838 novella *La Maison Nucingen*; then the fabricated 1846 pamphlet *Histoire édifiante et curieuse de Rothschild Ier, roi des juifs* by "Satan" (Georges Dairnvaell) — the origin point of the entire legend **[S: Britannica 2025; scholarship]**. The legend's institutional laundering is itself documented: the 1911 *Encyclopaedia Britannica* repeated it; Reeves's 1887 *The Rothschilds: The Financial Rulers of Nations* amplified it; Hollywood's 1934 *The House of Rothschild* dramatized it; Goebbels answered with the 1940 propaganda film *Die Rothschilds – Aktien von Waterloo*; even Allen Dulles's 1963 spy memoir repeated it. **The documented facts** (Nathan's courier at Ostend brought the news on the morning of 20 June 1815 and it went immediately to the government; Nathan's own first concern was the government's need, per the ODNB's authors) and **the market evidence** (the London interest rate series shows no anomalous movement around Waterloo — noted by Guy de Rothschild) kill the story. Ferguson dates the family's real Waterloo-era profits to honest, enormous, and boring: £1 million a month in bullion for Wellington's army, moved at risk. What Nathan earned from the victory was a knighthood's-worth of credit and the reputation that made the 1815 French indemnity business possible.

### 10.3 The Illuminati commission — **[X] → debunked, with the myth's own family tree**
**Claim:** Mayer Amschel commissioned Adam Weishaupt to found the Bavarian Illuminati in 1770 (or 1776) to infiltrate world finance. **Verdict:** false. Weishaupt founded the order **independently in 1776**; it was banned in 1784. The documented genealogy of the *myth*: Barruel's 1797–1806 conspiracy histories claimed a Jewish sect secretly ruled the secret societies; the accusation resurfaced in 1878 and was pushed by Drumont's *La France juive* (1886), which "outed" Weishaupt as a secret Jew; Nesta Webster and Lady Queenborough (*Occult Theocracy*, 1933) carried it into the twentieth century; the American Gerald Winrod's 1935 tract *Adam Weishaupt, a Human Devil* made the explicit claim that the Illuminati was "secretly directed by the Frankfurt-based industrialist Mayer Amschel Rothschild"; W.G. Carr repeated it. Even **National Geographic** once repeated the claim and was forced to publish a retraction: "we have found no historical evidence to back up that claim" **[S: NatGeo editor's note; scholarship]**. The myth's persistence mechanism: it needs no documents, because it explains why no documents exist.

The claim exceeds world GDP by an order of magnitude. ## PART 16 — THE HOUSE FILE (ARCHITECTURE, INTERIORS, AND FATES)

This part expands Part 6.7 and §5.1 with the full architectural and material record.

### 16.1 The Judengasse as a built environment

The ghetto that contained all three of the family's houses was a single street, roughly 330 meters long, built from 1460–62 against the old city wall on the city's eastern edge — the polluted corner where the fish market and tanneries stood **[S: Kracauer]**. Its plan was drawn in 1432 but took thirty years to execute; it was Europe's first walled Jewish quarter, predating Venice's by over fifty years **[B: METAhub]**. The 1462 intake was about 15–20 families (roughly 110 people); by 1520, about 250 people in 28 houses; by 1556, 550 in 60; by 1580, 1,200 in 120; by 1610, 2,700 in 195 houses — the largest and most important Jewish community in the German-speaking world from the sixteenth century on **[A: Dietz's demographic series; B: METAhub]**. By the eighteenth century, 3,000–4,000 people lived in a lane meant for a tenth that number — which is why occupancy statistics after the 1711 fire read 8,000 people at 41 per house in some counts, why the lane was widened (after 1711) to about 20 feet, and why three-story construction was mandated **[B: Kriegk]**.

The lane was administered internally by oligarchic "builders and coffer-masters," maintained three synagogues (later four), nine yeshivot at its height, a bathhouse, a clinic, and a bakery famous for *shalet* (the slow-cooked Sabbath stew) **[B: METAhub; S: Elon]**. It spoke **Western Yiddish** — the Judeo-German dialect written in Hebrew characters, called *Mauscheln* by hostile outsiders — and the family's business correspondence stayed in that script into the 1850s **[A: Fraiman]**. Five fires mark its history: **1711, 1719, 1721, 1774, 1796** **[S: Elon]**.

Two more textures from the record, preserved because they explain the psychology of the place: the houses' pictorial emblems — signs — appeared not only on keystones and facades but on utensils, rings, book-bindings, scarves, handkerchiefs, and **tombstones**; a family carried its sign through life and into the grave **[S: Elon]**. And after the Fettmilch expulsion of 1614–16, the community returned to the lane on 28 February 1616, led back through the city in a humiliating procession **to the beat of a drum** — the drummer being an Oppenheim, nicknamed "**Trumm**" (drum) — while Fettmilch and his fellow rebels were executed; the city paid **176,000 gulden compensation**, and the community kept **two annual festivals** of remembrance, with the *Megillat Vintz* (Elchanan bar Abraham's 1648 chronicle, and the *Vinz-Hans-Lied*) fixing the memory in liturgy and song **[B: Kriegk; S: scholarship on the Fettmilch era]**. The visibility rules, in their completed form: until 1726, Jews wore **two yellow rings sewn to their clothing** and women a **blue-striped veil**; market selling came only **after** Christians had sold; the Corpus Christi procession closed the lane entirely; Jewish petitioners used a **back entrance to the town hall**; peddling outside fair weeks was fined **[S/B: Elon; Streun; Kriegk]**. And the community's battles above the lane: sustained litigation before the **Reichshofrat (Vienna)** and the Imperial Chamber Court, 1765–90 — the files at the Frankfurt Jewish Community archive are the legal weather of Mayer's generation **[A/S — research lead]**.

That drummer's descendant was the Hanover banker Oppenheim in whose house Mayer Amschel trained — the nineteenth-century account explicitly calls the apprenticeship "the first occasion of the acquisition of the Rothschild wealth," meaning the coin expertise **[B: Kriegk]** — a rhetorical flourish, but a genealogically true connection.

### 16.2 The House at the Red Shield (Rotes Schild), Judengasse 69

- Location: southern half of the lane's west side; the number 69 comes from the 1759 numbering (houses were named, not numbered, before 1759) **[S: ADB]**.
- The ghetto register of **1616** already lists residents at the sign "zum Rothen Schild": "**Mosche zum rothen Schild** — **Isaak Rothschild [who had moved to] zum rothen Schwerdt**"; by **1715** the house is held by "**Gumprecht Trier im roten Schild**" **[S: ADB, quoting the Judenstättigkeit]**.
- The museum's house history (METAhub) dates its construction to **~1563** by "Isaac from the Roten Hahn," who hung a red sign on the new house "to show the connection" to his family's rooster-sign house next door **[B]**.
- After the family left, the house stayed with poorer relatives: **Joseph Rothschild**, a Hebrew book dealer, died there after the 1711 fire; he had no descendants, and the line at the Red Shield ends with him; the house was demolished in **1843** **[B: METAhub]**.
- A nuance worth stating precisely: the museum notes that "the Rothschild banking family never lived" at the Red Shield — true in a strict sense for the adult banking line (Moses Isaac the cantor lived at the *Gelber Ring*; the rabbinic line of Elchanan Hähnchen and Rabbi Isaac lived at the Red Shield itself) — but the banker's direct ancestors were born into this house-line, and the *name* they carried is this house **[B/A]**. The *Jewish Encyclopedia* adds a small puzzle — that the house "at first bore the sign of a green shield" — a claim we log here as an unresolved muddle in the JE's own account **[S]**.
- The ancestor table's geographic markers (Berghoeffer): the Red Shield at the southern end, west side; the Hinterpfann near the northern entrance, east side — the family's documented geography thus spanned the entire length of the lane **[A]**.

### 16.3 The Hinterpfann (Hinterhaus zur Pfanne), Judengasse 188

- A rear tenement of the Pfanne house ("at the pan," near the lane's north entrance); first mentioned **1552**, rebuilt **1601**, rebuilt again after the 1711 fire **[A: Berghoeffer; B: Streun]**.
- Dimensions: **1.70-meter facade**, three floors plus attic, about 120 m² total; Mayer's birthplace and his home for his first forty-two years **[B/S]**.
- Household: the Rothschilds shared it with the **Bauer family**; Elon's reconstruction has the two families at ten to twelve people; later descriptions allow periods of up to thirty under the roof when extended family squeezed in **[S/B]**.
- Interior textures (Corti's description of the Green Shield pattern applies to the Hinterpfann's arrangement as well): the dim office stacked with cotton and silk bales; a **rope handrail** on the stairs (rough enough to chafe hands); a **pull-up ladder** to the attic; **boarded attic windows** — an ordinance forbade ghetto Jews to look into Christian houses and gardens on the other side of the wall; ground-floor parental bedroom; children in one room **[B: Corti; S: Elon]**.

### 16.4 The House at the Green Shield (Grünes Schild), Judengasse 148 — the Stammhaus

- Built **1615**; 4.30 m facade (the city dictionary gives 4.70 m), 11.5 m deep, four stories; opposite the lane's middle western gate; "one of the largest houses in the ghetto" **[B: Fraiman; G]**.
- Purchase in two halves **1783 and 1785** (Berghoeffer, from the city archive); Hebrew contract with a **Sukkah clause**; possession and move **1786**; for the three intervening years the family rented another Judengasse home — that rental contract also survives (it was once in the Jewish Museum) **[A/B]**.
- Interior, from Corti and Fraiman: the family occupied the **right half** of a two-family building (three left-hand windows belonged to the neighbors); a five-sided convex **green shield** above the first door; an ancient doorbell; **Gudula's green room**; the **bridal wreath under glass**; a **secret cupboard behind a mirror**; a narrow creaking staircase with cupboards built into it; the **nine-by-nine-foot back room that was the first banking house** — a large iron chest opening from the back, **secret wall-shelves** behind panels; the kitchen 12 × 5 feet with a one-pot hearth; a **rooftop terrace** (used for the Sukkah, planted with greenery — "the only garden they were allowed," Elon notes bitterly, since Jews were barred from public gardens); their own water pump **[B/S]**.
- Family lore transmitted by Miriam Rothschild: all the children shared **one bedroom**; the house's 1930s photographs show modest contents despite the fortune behind them **[B: Fraiman]**. Trumbull's eyewitness description of the ghetto (1780s) adds the outside view: a crowded, dark lane — his estimate of 10,000 Jews was exaggerated; the real figure was 3,000–4,000, about a tenth of the city **[B: Fraiman, citing Trumbull]**.
- Neighbors: the **Schiff** family at the house "zur Arche" (at the ark — a carved ship), who ran a second-hand shop; the Green Shield stood "facing a small lane opposite the middle western gate," with the Schiffs two doors away **[B: Corti; Fraiman]**.
- The house had **two vaulted cellars** reached by a trapdoor, with **secret passages** between houses — a ghetto-wide survival architecture from pogrom times; neighboring houses' cellar doors connected, and Kriegk preserves the anecdote of antlers being smuggled house-to-house through them during guild searches **[B: Kriegk; Streun]**.
- Later history: the ghetto was demolished in stages (**1840; 1884–85**), the lane renamed **Börnestrasse** after Ludwig Börne (born Löb Baruch, 6 May 1786, at No. 118 — thirty doors from the Stammhaus; baptized 1818; the radical journalist whom Heine memorialized in the Hanukkah-walk anecdote, where Börne's speech on Laetitia is quoted at length by Fraiman) **[B]**.
- The Stammhaus legal chain (Berghoeffer, full): 1872 — Oppenheim's widow vacated; **9 August 1882** — city expropriation for 15,250 marks 80 pfennig; **4 October 1884** — cleared; **11 May 1885** — purchase contract (plot 1 Ar 91.50 qm; buildings 145–149; price 35,460 marks plus 1,000 marks); rebuilt 1886 by architect **Franz von Hoven** at Börnestrasse 26 under the testamentary foundation of **Amschel Mayer** (1,200,000 gulden for the poor Israelites of the community, plus a 50,000-gulden dowry fund; prayer meetings on the founders' death-days); 1888 — a Gumpertz Zuntz apartment in the building; location fixed on the Merian plan of 1711 **[A]**.
- The family's own bank had already moved out: from **1813** the banking house stood at the corner of Fahrgasse and the Judengasse (Fahrgasse 146), the corner house Mayer ordered built the day before he died, on a plot bought in 1809 in the northern, 1796-burned section of the lane **[B: Streun; Personenlexikon]**.
- **Destroyed by bombs on 18 May 1944** (Streun's correction of the widespread "the Stammhaus still survives" claim). What survives: the archaeological site — five ghetto house foundations excavated in 1987 during Battonnstraße 47 construction, over which the **Museum Judengasse** was built **[B: Streun; METAhub]**. The 1886 rebuilt Stammhaus was what the bombs destroyed; the medieval foundations in the museum are neighbors' and family houses of the lane.

## PART 17 — THE LEDGER FILE: EVERY DOCUMENTED TRANSACTION THREAD

This part expands Part 7 with the complete fine detail from Berghoeffer's chapters 1–3, Corti, and the numismatic literature.

### 17.1 The coin and antiquities business, year by year

- **1763–64:** the Landgrave's own records date the beginning of his coin collection to 1763; General von Estorff is Mayer's documented patron; in 1763 the crown prince hears of "a young Jew named Rothschild who had bought coins for him in Hanover" **[B: Corti]**.
- **October 1764 – December 1765:** the crown prince's privy-purse accounts (*Schatullkasse*) run; in **June 1765** they record a payment of **38 gulden 30 kreuzer to "a Jew named Meyer" for medals** — Berghoeffer's inference, explicitly flagged as inference, that this is Mayer Amschel; he supports it by noting the prince visited the Frankfurt fair three times in April 1765 and that a Hanau court Jew of similar name (Amschell Kahn) was a different man **[A/B]**.
- **1769–70:** Mayer's own business established (returned from Hanover 1764; joined his brother; court factor September 1769).
- **1770s–80s:** ten surviving catalogues (small octavo, 8–16 pages), six from the 1770s (sales 1,666–2,917 gulden), four from the 1780s (2,579–5,074 gulden); the 1770s stock is mostly thalers, the 1780s add Greek and Roman material; the catalogues went to **Duke Karl August of Weimar** (Goethe's patron), **Karl Theodor, Elector Palatine**, and the Landgrave; the model was proto-mail-order — catalogues posted, selections marked, goods sent on approval, the rest returned; a 1789 transaction with Karl Theodor exemplifies the cycle; **Madai's Talerkabinett** was the standard price reference; Frankfurt-fair auctions were the supply floor **[A: Berghoeffer; B: CoinsWeekly]**.
- **1778:** first address-book listing — "antiques, medals and curiosities" **[A]**.
- **1780:** letter to Bertuch of Weimar shows the antiquities trade in full flow **[A]**.
- **1783/84:** acquisition of "antique carved figures"; **1786:** an "antique stone cabinet" **[A]**.
- **1789:** a surviving letter about English bills shows the parallel finance business; medal deliveries to the prince resume and continue until the French occupation **[A]**.
- **1791:** last peacetime medal deliveries; **1796:** the servant Hirsch Liebmann arrested for stealing 200+ gold and silver medals (the accidental inventory) **[A]**.
- **1807–09 — the medal-cabinet rescue:** after the French seizure of the Hessian treasures, the elector in exile ordered the repurchase of his dispersed medal cabinet, with strict price caps (6 gulden per ducat for gold; 2 gulden 20 kreuzer per Loth for silver). Executed through the family's channels: **1807** — 941 silver medals, 168 marks weight, 6,272 gulden; **26 February 1808** — 92 gold and 1,320 silver pieces; by January 1809 Buderus had settled **23,053 gulden 20 kreuzer** for the operation. Posthumously, deliveries continued: **January 1813** (13 gold, 208 silver pieces, via Prague) and **1814** (434 thalers). In 1813 the elector even bought back through the firm a ring set with a large reddish diamond **[A: Berghoeffer, from the prince's and Buderus's papers]**.

### 17.2 The goods trade, consignment by consignment (1796–1810)

- **1796:** English suppliers Bradforth (Yorkshire) and Sisson Wood & Co (Leeds) — cloth; warehouse rent 400 fl 24 kr/year.
- **1798:** Nathan to Manchester precisely *because* England was the main source — "there was not enough room in the city" for the next generation, as Nathan later put it; he left Frankfurt on the Thursday after an insult on the Tuesday (his own retrospective to Buxton, February 1834, thirty-six years on) **[A/B]**.
- **1802:** Konrad Knopf, Nuremberg — batiste, muslin. Same years: local houses Sichel & Hildesheimer (Frankfurt), Lob Hertz (Mainz).
- **May–August 1803:** chests to C. Lachmann & Co (Brussels), Jacob Marius (Amsterdam), Veuve Jalon (Lyon); Carl already at Dunkirk on family business **[A]**.
- **July 1806:** Starman & Co, London — 14 bales coffee, 6 chests + 3 seroons indigo, ship *Mercurius*, Captain Jacobs, to Hamburg.
- **1807:** Hamburg cotton; **1809:** sugar and tobacco; **1810:** cloth, hare skins, sheep's wool, arrack, wine; Macedonian cotton; Prague houses.
- **1809–10 detail:** £6,023.16.0 in cambrics to Gosch & Lotes (Amsterdam) for "James Mayer"; 900 dozen cloths at 9,700 fl returning 2,229 fl 2 kr profit — **23%**; August 1809: 15 chests indigo, £1,230.10.6, from Echenique & Co; November 1809: 150 packs of twilled bandanas; July 1810: multiple shipments totaling **£45,000+** (mule yarn, sugar, coffee, water twist, muslin handkerchiefs, cambrics, printed cambrics). Nathan personally insured six chest shipments August 1801–August 1802 via S. S. London, Bury Street; insurance ran **35–50% of cargo value** — the measurable fear of the war years **[A]**.
- **The end of the goods era:** the Continental System chronology — 1786 Eden Treaty baseline; 16 May 1806 English blockade Brest–Elbe; **21 November 1806 Berlin Decree**; **5 August 1810 Trianon tariff** (40–50%); spy Thiard in Frankfurt August 1810; two French regiments occupy Frankfurt October 1810; **Fontainebleau decrees (14/23 October 1810)** — sequestration and public burning; **234 Frankfurt firms ruined**, roughly 100,000 florins of textile damage across the trade; the Rothschilds' declared English goods: **60,000 fl, of which 27,000 fl was indigo**; records burned (documented loss 60,000 fl in paper plus a 20,000-franc fine); by 1812 the goods trade was wound down and the 1810 deed's asset list is bills, bonds, state paper, wine, cash — a bank's balance sheet **[A]**.
- Berghoeffer's synthesis, to be quoted rather than paraphrased: the goods trade "from the beginning until 1810 was the actual foundation of his business" — the bank, as with Bethmann (from Adami, 1748), Metzler, Hollweg, and Grunelius, grew out of the trading house **[A]**.

### 17.3 Bills, the Elector, and the war-finance mechanics (1790–1813)

- **1790:** Buderus–Schnapper dealings documented; Schnapper introduces Buderus to Mayer; November 1790 — the first documented draft to Rothschild: 2,000 **Laubtaler** **[B: Corti]**.
- **1794:** the elector's English investments to be repatriated; Buderus proposed Rothschild alongside Bethmann and four other firms; Wilhelm chose Bethmann alone — "but it did not happen again" **[B]**.
- **1798:** the elector's English business passes to Rothschild handling single-handedly (the *Jewish Encyclopedia*'s account of the bill-discounting ladder: 1789 changing English gold; 1794 £150,000 shared with six bullion brokers; by end-1798, single-handed) **[S]**.
- **1800–06:** documented handling of 1,750,000 thaler at 4% for the elector; the wine-cask legend's documentary core — funds physically hidden, interest scrupulously paid on English bank deposits **[S: JE; ADB]**.
- **1806–07:** the elector flees; the fortune stays. Streun's correction of the legend: what Mayer kept at home were **four chests of documents** — the archive of the relationship — not bags of gold; the money worked through the sons' networks **[B]**.
- **1808–12:** the documented apex — servicing the English subsidy flow to Wellington's armies: **£600,000** of the elector's money entrusted to Nathan; **£800,000 in East India Company gold** for Wellington moving through the same channels; four parallel profit streams (deposit interest, exchange, commission, own-loan placement); 1813–15: **£15,000,000** moved to the Continent through the brothers, at **£1,000,000 a month** in 1815; nine elector transactions totaling **£664,850** by 1813 at **⅛%** commission **[S/A]**.
