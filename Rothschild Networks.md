## PART 1 — THE AMERICAN AXIS: A CENTURY OF AGENCY, AND THE MYTH THAT GREW OVER IT

### 1.1 The Belmont century (documented)

The Rothschilds never built a permanent American branch — that failure is documented and analyzed in Volume I and again in Part 6 below. But "no branch" is not "no America." The documented record shows a **hundred-year agency business** conducted through correspondents:

- **August Belmont** (born August Schönberg, 1813–1890) trained in the Frankfurt office in **1829–30**, was sent to Naples, then dispatched to New York in **1836** to become the firm's agent — the letters he carried are in the Rothschild Archive, and the Massachusetts Historical Society holds the matching American side of the correspondence **[A: Rothschild Archive; MassHist]**.
- Belmont built the agency into a bank (August Belmont & Co.), served as US representative of the family's interests, and became a Democratic powerbroker (chairman of the Democratic National Committee, 1860–1872). His Rothschild letters run for decades — the Archive's "American Letter Books" preserve the firm's side: **112 volumes of letter-books, 1834–1918**, plus **51 volumes of American accounts, 1831–1918** **[A: Rothschild Archive holdings]**.
- The agency business was not ceremonial. Documented correspondents and agents included **J. L. & S. Joseph & Co.** (London–New York), **Aaron Palmer**, **Benjamin Davidson** (San Francisco agent, 1853–1862), **Chieves & Osborne / R. - The commodities tell the story: American **cotton and tobacco** flowed through the family's European finance for a century — the same trade Volume I documents as the foundation of the Frankfurt house, transplanted to the slave-economy staples of the antebellum South. We flag this plainly: the agency network financed and moved the proceeds of slave-grown cotton, as did every European house in the trade; the documented counterpoint is that the London house **declined business in Texas slave-state bonds** in the 1830s (see §5.9).

## PART 2 — STATE CAPTURE AND SOVEREIGN ADVISORY

### 2.1 Bleichröder: the proxy model (documented, and the century's clearest case)

How does a bank without a branch in a capital hold power there? The nineteenth century's documented answer is **Gerson von Bleichröder** (1822–1893) — the Rothschilds' Berlin agent, where the family deliberately kept no house.

- Bismarck's personal banker for **three decades**; handler of the chancellor's private accounts, stock options, and **4% mortgages on Bismarck's estates**; the man who "handled tax inspectors" for the Bismarck family **[S: Fritz Stern, *Gold and Iron*; A. J. P. Taylor, NYRB]**.
- **Off-budget war finance:** when the Prussian Diet refused funds for the 1866 war, the Rothschild bank — operating through Bleichröder — underwrote the state's borrowing on the market (the Seehandlung operation, summer 1865); during the Franco-Prussian War the family took an equal share (**3 million thaler**) in the war loan **[S]**.
- **The secret payments:** Bleichröder disbursed the funds that induced **Ludwig II of Bavaria** to press the imperial crown on William I — the documented instance of Rothschild-channel money moving a crown **[S: Stern]**.
- **Versailles:** Bleichröder handled the technical side of the French indemnity; the 1872 **indemnity loan** made the family's position; he was ennobled (1872), decorated with the Iron Cross, and seated at the Congress of Berlin (1878), where he cooperated with Montefiore and Crémieux on the Romanian-Jewish clauses **[S]**.
- **The conflict-of-interest texture, documented in the primary record:** Bleichröder promoted a Russian loan *against Bismarck's direction* (Bismarck's marginal note: "Who would?"); he routinely traded his own book against his clients' information; the relationship ended with Bismarck's son Herbert calling him "the filthy Jew" — antisemitism as the court Jews' permanent terminal risk **[S]**.

**Pattern, stated once and reused:** the family held power in capitals where it had no office **through a dependent agent embedded in the state's private finances** — Bleichröder in Berlin, Buderus in Kassel (Volume I), later Bleichröder's successors. The model's weak point is equally documented: the agent's exposure is personal, and the protection is revocable.

### 2.2 The 1825 lifeline: central banks on the family wire (documented)

A single documented episode anchors the "control of central banks" conversation in fact: in the **1825 crisis**, the **Bank of France supplied the Bank of England £400,000 in gold**, and the **Rothschilds facilitated the transfer** — a documented instance of the family as the operational channel between the era's two great central banks **[A: Bank of England historical records; Volume I's JE/ADB documentation]**. The family did not own the Bank of England (the "Rothschild owns the BoE" claim is **[X]**, falsified by the Bank's public ownership structure); it *serviced* central banks — a subtler and more interesting position.

Rothschild via a tri-partite commission** — the family's position inside the Egyptian fiscal machine documented in the thesis record; the intermediary layer (the Khedive's personal banker Dervieu) is where the documented corruption texture lives **[S: CUNY thesis; PP papers]**.
- The scholarly verdict, quoted from the thesis record: "**for the Rothschilds, the British government represented a door into an increasingly British Egypt open to more lucrative loans**" — the symbiosis of state and bank stated by the documentation itself **[S]**.
